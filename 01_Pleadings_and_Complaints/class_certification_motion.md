### II. COMPLIANCE WITH FED. R. CIV. P. 23(a) PREREQUISITES

#### 1. Numerosity (Rule 23(a)(1))

The proposed Master Class and Subclasses comprise thousands of geographically dispersed individuals across the United States, Latin America, and Europe. With nearly 6,000 certified corporate and individual claimants registered under the FCSC dockets alone—and tens of thousands of descendants of antebellum latifundia victims—joinder of all members is manifestly impracticable.

#### 2. Commonality (Rule 23(a)(2))

Common questions of law and fact unite the Class. Under *Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes*, class claims must depend upon a common contention capable of class-wide resolution. Here, the core issues include:

* *Whether* the continuous 221-year chain of U.S. sovereign actions (Vectors 1–1805) constitutes a single "Continuing Wrongful Act" under international law (ARSIWA Article 14) and federal common law, defeating the 6-year Tucker Act time-bar;

* *Whether* the military occupations (1899–1902, 1906–1909) and the imposition of the Platt Amendment created an active, non-repudiated fiduciary trust relationship;

* *Whether* the systematic application of economic warfare directives (Proclamation 3355, 3447, OFAC regulations) constitutes an uncompensated Fifth Amendment inverse condemnation.

#### 3. Typicality (Rule 23(a)(3))

The claims of the Named Plaintiffs are typical of the class members' claims. Named Plaintiffs hold direct, chain-of-title succession rights deriving from dispossessed sugar estates and urban assets impacted by the exact executive orders, banking receiverships, and statutory blockades challenged by every absent class member.

#### 4. Adequacy of Representation (Rule 23(a)(4))

Named Plaintiffs have no interests antagonistic to or in conflict with absent class members. They are represented by seasoned class litigation counsel with deep expertise in complex federal jurisdiction, archival discovery, and cross-border restitution jurisprudence.

### III. CERTIFICATION IS PROPER UNDER RULES 23(b)(2) AND 23(b)(3)

#### A. Rule 23(b)(2): Injunctive and Declaratory Relief

Defendants have acted and refused to act on grounds generally applicable to the entire Class. Final declaratory relief—specifically declaring that Defendants bear continuous structural legal responsibility and that the historical claims are not time-barred by virtue of the active sanctions continuum—is appropriate and necessary with respect to the Class as a whole.

#### B. Rule 23(b)(3): Predominance and Superiority

Common legal questions regarding federal sovereign liability, the continuing wrongs exception to 28 U.S.C. § 2501, and fiduciary breach overwhelmingly predominate over individual questions of property valuation. Class adjudication is clearly superior to thousands of individual suits, preserving judicial resources and avoiding conflicting adjudications regarding the 221-year continuum.
