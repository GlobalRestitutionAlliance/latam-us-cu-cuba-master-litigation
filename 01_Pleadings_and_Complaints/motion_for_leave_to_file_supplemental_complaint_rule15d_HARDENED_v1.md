### I. STATUTORY BASIS UNDER RULE 15(d)

#### A. Pleading Transactions and Events Occurring After Initial Filing

Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(d) authorizes the court, on just terms, to permit a party to serve a supplemental pleading setting out any transaction, occurrence, or event that happened after the date of the pleading to be supplemented. Under United States v. Dickinson, 331 U.S. 745 (1947), continuing administrative takings are properly supplemented.

#### B. Active 2026 OFAC Blocking Orders

This motion supplements the record with specific, ongoing 2026 administrative enforcement actions by OFAC under 31 C.F.R. Part 515, including recent civil monetary penalties, blocking of international bank transactions, and retention of accrued interest on blocked accounts.

### II. DESTRUCTION OF STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS BAR

#### A. Continuous Resetting of the 28 U.S.C. § 2501 Clock

Under ARSIWA Article 14(2), each fresh act of enforcement constitutes a continuing breach. By supplementing the complaint with 2026 enforcement events, Plaintiffs establish that the cause of action accrued within days of the current filing, rendering the six-year limitation in 28 U.S.C. § 2501 indisputably satisfied.

#### B. Independent Actionable Injury

Each modern blocking of funds causes fresh economic injury in fact to Class members holding beneficial interests in those accounts.
