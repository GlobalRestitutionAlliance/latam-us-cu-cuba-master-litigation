## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Architecture

Campaign 64 establishes an exhaustive evidentiary cross-walk matrix targeting the state-controlled commercial retail monopolies of the Republic of Cuba and the military holding conglomerate GAESA (Grupo de Administración Empresarial S.A.), specifically **Corporación CIMEX S.A.** (operating Cadena de Tiendas Panamericanas) and **Tiendas Caribe** (formerly TRD - Tiendas Recuperadoras de Divisas). Together, these two state entities hold an absolute, non-competitive sovereign monopoly over retail department stores, consumer electronics, food supermarkets, and apparel distribution across the island. 

The historical and legal predicate of this commercial retail network is anchored directly in the mass confiscations of American and allied commercial enterprises under **Law 890 (October 14, 1960)**. Through Law 890, the Cuban state seized without compensation 382 major private industrial and commercial enterprises, including the nationwide retail networks of **Sears, Roebuck and Co.** (certified by the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission under Claim No. CU-2494) and **F.W. Woolworth Co.** (FCSC Claim No. CU-2496), as well as Cuba's premier luxury department store, **El Encanto** (Solís, Entrialgo y Cía). These confiscated department stores and commercial real estate were absorbed directly into state retail networks, and later transferred into the commercial corporate umbrella of GAESA and CIMEX.

In recent years, the Cuban state restructured this retail apparatus into **Tiendas MLC** (Moneda Libremente Convertible), systematically denying Cuban citizens the right to purchase basic consumer goods, food, and appliances in national currency, and demanding payment exclusively via magnetic debit cards denominated in foreign currencies (USD, Euros). These transactions are routed through state-operated payment gateways and electronic clearing rails, specifically **FINCIMEX S.A.** and **Orbit S.A.**, with the retail inventory supplied through offshore front companies and logistics intermediaries situated in the **Colon Free Zone (Zona Libre de Colón, Panama)**.

Under the commercial activity exception of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)), the expropriation exception (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)), the alter ego doctrine of *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), and the landmark precedent of *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX S.A.*, No. 19-cv-1277 (D.D.C.), neither CIMEX nor Tiendas Caribe enjoy sovereign immunity. Their commercial assets, POS clearing escrows, Panama trade credits, and foreign bank deposits are fully subject to post-judgment turnover, garnishment, and maritime attachment under 28 U.S.C. § 1610, Fed. R. Civ. P. 69, and N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5225.

## 4. Wave-by-Wave Legal Analysis & Enforcement Strategy

### Wave 1: Expropriated Pre-1959 American Department Stores, Retail Chains & FCSC Claims ($85.00B)
- **FCSC Certified Claims Foundation:** Enacted on October 14, 1960, Law 890 seized private commercial networks overnight without compensation. Sears, Roebuck and Co. operated extensive, multi-story department stores across Havana (including its flagship store on Avenida de Simón Bolívar / Reina), Santa Clara, Camagüey, and Santiago de Cuba, certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2494. F.W. Woolworth Co. operated an 8-store variety store network across Havana and provincial capitals, certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2496. Alongside El Encanto, these commercial retail institutions formed the premier department store real estate of Cuba.
- **Title and Property Continuity:** The physical properties, warehouses, and urban retail parcels seized under Law 890 were never returned or compensated. They were converted directly into state department stores under MINCIN and later incorporated into Cadena de Tiendas Panamericanas (CIMEX) and Tiendas Caribe. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3) and Helms-Burton Title III (22 U.S.C. § 6082), these properties represent active trafficking in confiscated property, supporting statutory treble damages and worldwide asset tracing.

### Wave 2: Corporación CIMEX S.A., Cadena Panamericanas & Sovereign Retail Monopolies ($75.00B)
- **Piercing the CIMEX Corporate Veil:** Corporación CIMEX S.A. is the commercial linchpin of the Cuban state apparatus, operating under GAESA military control. In *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX S.A.*, No. 19-cv-1277 (D.D.C.), federal courts affirmed that CIMEX engages in commercial activities without entitlement to sovereign immunity. CIMEX's Cadena de Tiendas Panamericanas operates thousands of retail stores, supermarkets, and specialty boutiques nationwide.
- **Commercial Disgorgement:** Under the alter ego doctrine of *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), the separate corporate status of CIMEX must be disregarded. All commercial retail markups, wholesale import margins, and central logistics operations (such as the Berroa Logistics Terminal in Havana) are reachable for post-judgment satisfaction under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2).

### Wave 3: Tiendas Caribe (TRD), Military Logistics & Commercial Retailing ($70.00B)
- **Military Retail Apparatus:** Tiendas Caribe (originally Tiendas Recuperadoras de Divisas - TRD) was established under MINFAR and GAESA direct management to capture foreign currency directly from the civilian population. Operating thousands of neighborhood commercial outlets, boutique chains, and airport duty-free stores, TRD functions as a pure commercial enterprise.
