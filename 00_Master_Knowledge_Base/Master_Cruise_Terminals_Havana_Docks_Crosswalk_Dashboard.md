## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Legal Architecture
Campaign 63 operationalizes an offensive multi-jurisdictional post-judgment execution campaign targeting **$325.00 Billion** in sovereign commercial maritime passenger cruise terminals, historic deepwater piers, international cruise line trafficking liabilities, passenger head tax clearing accounts, and shore excursion revenue escrows. Under the command of the Omni-Counsel Architect, this campaign focuses on the crown-jewel passenger terminal assets of the Port of Havana and regional cruise ports (Cienfuegos and Santiago de Cuba), operated under the command of military holding company **GAESA**, port holding monopoly **Almacenes Universales S.A. (AUSA)**, and **Empresa de Servicios Portuarios de Occidente**.

The campaign bridges one of the most prominent, successful federal court litigation victories under Title III of the LIBERTAD (Helms-Burton) Act with sovereign asset restitution:
1. **The Havana Docks Corporation Certified Claim (FCSC Claim No. CU-2492):** On October 24, 1960, the Cuban Revolutionary Government confiscated the 99-year concession and deepwater finger piers (Muelle San Francisco, Muelle La Machina, Muelle Santa Clara) constructed and owned by the **Havana Docks Corporation**. In 1971, the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) certified Claim No. CU-2492, verifying uncompensated losses that established an unassailable property right under U.S. law.
2. **The S.D. Fla. Helms-Burton Title III Judgments ($440M+ Precedents):** In *Havana Docks Corp. v. Carnival Corp.*, *Havana Docks Corp. v. Royal Caribbean Cruises, Ltd.*, *Havana Docks Corp. v. Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings, Ltd.*, and *Havana Docks Corp. v. MSC Cruises S.A.*, the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida (Judge Beth Bloom) entered historic judgments totaling over **$440 Million** in statutory treble damages, pre-judgment interest, and attorneys' fees, holding that commercial cruise lines knowingly and intentionally 'trafficked' in the confiscated piers by disembarking passenger vessels and paying docking fees to Cuban state enterprises.
3. **Passenger Head Taxes & Port Usage Levies:** Every cruise vessel passenger calling at Havana was assessed mandatory port usage and head taxes ($10 to $25 per passenger) collected by AUSA and swept into hard-currency accounts at Banco Financiero Internacional (BFI). These sovereign commercial revenues are non-immune assets subject to execution under 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2) and § 1610(a)(2).
4. **Global Cruise Conglomerate Clearinghouses:** The major global cruise operators maintain multi-billion-dollar corporate banking reserves, ticket float accounts, and passenger merchant processing escrows in Florida, New York, London, Geneva, and Panama, providing liquid, enforceable targets for post-judgment garnishment under Fed. R. Civ. P. 69 and Supplemental Admiralty Rule B.
