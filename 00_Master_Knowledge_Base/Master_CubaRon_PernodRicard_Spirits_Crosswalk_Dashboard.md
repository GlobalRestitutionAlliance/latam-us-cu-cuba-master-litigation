### Strategic Mandate & Legal Foundations

On October 14, 1960, under Law 890, the revolutionary regime confiscated without compensation the physical distilleries, aging bodegas, rectification plants, and world-renowned trademarks of **José Arechabala S.A. (JASA)** in Cárdenas, Matanzas, which had produced and owned the **Havana Club** rum brand since 1934. The Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) certified the expropriation under Claim No. CU-2499. The regime also confiscated the vast distilling infrastructure and aging cellars of **Bacardi Corp.** and **Matusalem (Ron Santiago)**.

In 1993, the Cuban state commercial monopoly (now **Corporación Cuba Ron S.A.**) entered into a 50/50 worldwide commercial joint venture with French beverage giant **Pernod Ricard S.A.** (Euronext Paris: RI) to establish **Havana Club International S.A. (HCI S.A.)** and holding company **Havana Club Holding S.A. (Luxembourg)**. HCI S.A. distributes millions of cases of Havana Club rum globally across Europe, Asia, Latin America, and international travel retail duty-free channels, generating hundreds of millions of dollars in net commercial revenues and distributing tens of millions in annual dividends to Cuba Ron S.A.

This Campaign deploys comprehensive post-judgment remedies under:
1. **Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA) Commercial Activity Exceptions (28 U.S.C. §§ 1605(a)(2), 1610(a)(2)):** The commercial distillation, aging, global bottling, maritime export, and worldwide wholesale of Havana Club rum constitute non-immune commercial activities carried on outside the United States with substantial commercial effects within U.S. jurisdiction and commerce.
2. **First National City Bank v. Bancec (462 U.S. 611) Alter Ego Unity:** Corporación Cuba Ron S.A. is an alter ego and dominated instrumentality of the Council of Ministers and the Ministry of the Food Industry (MINAL), permitting piercing of the corporate veil to satisfy judgments against the Republic of Cuba.
3. **Helms-Burton Act Title III Private Rights of Action (22 U.S.C. § 6082):** Pernod Ricard S.A. and HCI S.A. commercially exploit, traffic in, and benefit from confiscated property originally owned by José Arechabala S.A. and its living American heirs, triggering statutory treble damages in U.S. federal district courts (*Arechabala v. Pernod Ricard S.A.*, *Gomez Cabrera v. Pernod Ricard*).
4. **Section 211 Statutory Trademark Invalidation (Pub. L. 105-277, § 211):** Explicitly prohibits U.S. courts and the USPTO from recognizing, enforcing, or validating any trademark registration that is identical or substantially similar to a trademark used in connection with a confiscated business or asset, affirming the invalidity of Cuban state trademark assertions (*Havana Club Holding, S.A. v. Galleon S.A.*, 203 F.3d 116 (2d Cir. 2000)).
5. **Helms-Burton Act Title IV Mandatory Sanctions (22 U.S.C. § 6091):** Mandatory visa revocations and entry exclusions for corporate officers and controlling shareholders of multinational spirits combines trafficking in confiscated Cuban rum distilleries.
6. **Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA § 201) & 28 U.S.C. § 1610(g):** Attaching Pernod Ricard dividend disbursements, Luxembourg holding company royalties, European bonded customs inventory, and commercial bank clearance floats for deposit into the Master QSF Trust.
7. **26 U.S.C. § 468B Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF) Trust Distribution:** Directing all recovered commercial spirits proceeds into a Delaware statutory fiduciary trust for pro-rata distribution to the heirs of José Arechabala and certified claimants.
