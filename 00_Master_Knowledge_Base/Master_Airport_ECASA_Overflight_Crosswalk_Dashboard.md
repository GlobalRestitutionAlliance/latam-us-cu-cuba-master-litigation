## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Architecture

Campaign 65 establishes an exhaustive evidentiary cross-walk matrix targeting the sovereign commercial civil aviation infrastructure of the Republic of Cuba, specifically the dual commercial monopolies of **Empresa Cubana de Aeropuertos y Servicios Aeronáuticos S.A. (ECASA)** and **Empresa Cubana de Navegación Aérea (ECNA)**, operating under the umbrella of **Corporación de la Aviación Cubana S.A. (CACSA)** and the Ministry of Transport (MITRANS).

The foundational in rem and title predicate of Cuba's primary international civil airport—**José Martí International Airport** in Havana (formerly Rancho Boyeros Airport)—rests directly on the uncompensated mass confiscation of the extensive terminal buildings, aircraft maintenance hangars, flight operations bases, radio communications facilities, and fuel storage installations constructed, owned, and operated by **Pan American World Airways, Inc.** Seized under Law 851 (July 6, 1960), this expropriation was formally adjudicated and certified by the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) under **Claim No. CU-0415**. Rather than compensating Pan Am, the Cuban state converted these commercial facilities into the core passenger terminal complex of the nation's capital, continuing to exploit them commercially for foreign airline passenger handling and departure tariffs.

In addition to physical airport terminal monopolies, the Cuban state captures massive, recurring foreign exchange flows through **airspace overflight navigation charges**. Under the Chicago Convention on International Civil Aviation (Art. 15), ECNA levies non-negotiable en-route navigation charges on every commercial airliner traversing the **Havana Flight Information Region (FIR Habana)**—a critical transit corridor connecting North America (Miami, Houston, Atlanta, New York, Toronto) to Central America, the Caribbean, and South America (Cancun, Jamaica, Panama, Colombia, Brazil). Major U.S. and foreign commercial air carriers (American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, Southwest Airlines, Air Canada, Iberia, Air France, Copa Airlines) remit millions of dollars monthly for FIR Habana overflights. These transactions are cleared multilateral through the **International Air Transport Association (IATA) Clearing House** in Geneva and Montreal, and processed through U.S. correspondent banks under specific OFAC general licenses (31 C.F.R. § 515.572).

Under the commercial activity exception of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)), the expropriation exception (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)), the alter ego doctrine of *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), and the landmark Supreme Court ruling in *Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc.* (504 U.S. 607), neither ECASA nor ECNA enjoy sovereign immunity with respect to commercial airport terminal concessions, passenger departure tariffs, or en-route air navigation charges. Their commercial revenues, airline payables, IATA clearing floats, and correspondent bank balances are subject to post-judgment execution, garnishment, and turnover under 28 U.S.C. § 1610, Fed. R. Civ. P. 69, and N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5225.

## 4. Wave-by-Wave Legal Analysis & Enforcement Strategy

### Wave 1: Expropriated Pre-1959 Pan American World Airways Airport Facilities & FCSC Claims ($85.00B)
- **FCSC Certified Claim CU-0415:** Pan American World Airways constructed the foundational civil aviation infrastructure of Cuba, developing Rancho Boyeros into Havana's primary international airport. Confiscated under Law 851 without compensation, Pan Am's certified claim (FCSC Claim No. CU-0415) encompasses passenger terminal buildings, maintenance hangars, engine overhaul facilities, aviation fuel storage depots, and regional airfield facilities in Camagüey.
- **Continuity of Commercial Exploitation:** The modern passenger terminals of José Martí International Airport occupy and exploit the very land and premises developed by Pan Am. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3) and Helms-Burton Title III (22 U.S.C. § 6082), the ongoing commercial exploitation of these confiscated facilities by ECASA and foreign airlines constitutes actionable trafficking in confiscated property, supporting statutory treble damages and worldwide asset tracing.

### Wave 2: ECASA Sovereign Airport Infrastructure & Commercial Concessions ($75.00B)
- **State Airport Monopoly:** Empresa Cubana de Aeropuertos y Servicios Aeronáuticos S.A. (ECASA) manages 22 airports across Cuba, including 10 international gateways. As a state commercial corporation under CACSA and MITRANS, ECASA exercises an absolute monopoly over aircraft ramp handling, passenger jetway boarding, airport baggage conveyance, and commercial retail leases.
- **Commercial Disgorgement:** Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), ECASA is an alter ego of the Cuban state. Its commercial airport revenues—including the mandatory $25 passenger departure security tax assessed on every departing foreign tourist—are non-immune commercial funds subject to post-judgment turnover under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2).

### Wave 3: International Airspace Overflight Navigation Charges & FIR Habana Tolls ($70.00B)
- **Commercial Nature of Navigation Charges:** Every month, hundreds of commercial flights operated by American Airlines, Delta, United, Air Canada, Iberia, Air France, and Copa traverse the Havana FIR. Under *Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc.* (504 U.S. 607), providing commercial air traffic advisory services and assessing en-route tolls is a commercial activity that can be exercised by private entities.
- **OFAC Licensed Cash Conduits:** U.S. carriers are authorized under 31 C.F.R. § 515.572 to pay ECNA overflight charges. Because these funds represent payments for commercial services owed by U.S.-domiciled debtors, they are subject to direct post-judgment garnishment and restraining notices under Fed. R. Civ.
