### Strategic Mandate & Legal Foundations

Cuba's entire premium tobacco and luxury hand-rolled cigar industry operates as a state-dominated commercial combine through **Grupo Empresarial de Tabaco de Cuba (TABACUBA)** and its international marketing joint venture **Corporación Habanos S.A.** In 2020, Imperial Brands sold its 50% non-Cuban equity stake in Habanos S.A. and Tabacalera to a Hong Kong-based investment syndicate, **Allied Cigar Corporation**, for €1.22 Billion ($1.44 Billion USD). Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), Habanos S.A. functions as the commercial alter ego of the Cuban Ministry of Foreign Trade and Investment (MINCEX) and the Council of Ministers, generating hundreds of millions of dollars in net hard-currency annual profits through exclusive territorial distributorship monopolies across Europe, the Middle East, and Asia.

The core tangible and intangible assets underpinning this luxury monopoly—including world-famous cigar brands, landmark Havana rolling manufactories, curing barns, and the prime agricultural tobacco vegas of Vuelta Abajo, Pinar del Río—were expropriated without compensation from pre-1959 owners under Law 890 (October 14, 1960). In landmark federal and Supreme Court jurisprudence, the lawful title of original trademark owners and their rights to commercial accounts receivable have been repeatedly upheld against Cuban government intervention:
1. **Alfred Dunhill of London, Inc. v. Republic of Cuba, 425 U.S. 682 (1976):** Affirming *Menéndez v. Faber, Coe & Gregg, Inc.*, 345 F. Supp. 527 (S.D.N.Y. 1972) / 485 F.2d 1355 (2d Cir. 1973), establishing the commercial activity exception to the act of state doctrine. Expropriated cigar manufactory owners retain full ownership of pre- and post-nationalization trademark rights and export accounts receivable located outside Cuban borders.
2. **Foreign Claims Settlement Commission Certified Awards:** Certified claims CU-1335 (Menéndez, García y Cía., Ltda. - H. Upmann, Montecristo) and CU-1336 (Cifuentes y Cía. - Partagás, Ramón Allones) establish unassailable legal proof of uncompensated confiscation of historic rolling factories (Calle Amistad 407, Calle Industria 520) and tobacco plantations.
3. **FSIA Commercial Exceptions (28 U.S.C. §§ 1605(a)(2), 1610(a)(2)):** The commercial export of luxury hand-rolled cigars, licensing of global franchise chains (La Casa del Habano), collection of wholesale distributor royalties, and participation in international luxury auctions constitute non-immune commercial activities with direct effects in the United States and global commerce.
4. **Helms-Burton Act Title III Private Rights of Action (22 U.S.C. § 6082):** Transnational distributors, duty-free retailers, and trading syndicates trafficking in confiscated cigar trademarks, curing barns, and rolling facilities are subject to federal court jurisdiction for statutory treble damages.
5. **Helms-Burton Act Title IV Mandatory Sanctions (22 U.S.C. § 6091):** Mandatory visa revocations and entry exclusions for corporate officers and controlling directors of foreign tobacco combines trafficking in confiscated Cuban cigar marks and physical manufactories.
6. **Supplemental Admiralty Rule B & Free-Trade Zone Attachments:** Maritime containers of finished premium cigars in transit, ocean freight bills of lading, and bonded stocks stored in free-trade logistics hubs (Panama Colón Free Zone, Geneva Freeport, Madrid-Barajas) are subject to maritime and customs attachment.
7. **Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) § 201 & 28 U.S.C. § 1610(g) Garnishments:** Direct post-judgment execution against hard-currency tobacco clearing funds, Festival del Habano luxury humidor auction accounts, and distributor wholesale receivables held in European and Asian correspondent banks.
8. **Master Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF) Trust (26 U.S.C. § 468B):** Restitution architecture marshaling all liquidated tobacco receipts, royalty attachments, and Title III judgments into a court-supervised fund for certified claimants and heirs.

### Master Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF) Trust Distribution Architecture

All proceeds recovered under Campaign 77—including distributor receivables garnished under TRIA § 201, luxury cigar pallet attachments under Supplemental Admiralty Rule B, bonded free-zone seizures, Festival del Habano humidor auction escrows, and Helms-Burton Title III statutory damages—shall be marshaled directly into the court-supervised **Master Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF) Trust** established pursuant to **26 U.S.C. § 468B** and **Treasury Regulation § 1.468B-1**.

The QSF Administrator shall execute distributions under a transparent, pro-rata allocation matrix:
1. **Tier 1 (Certified Pre-1959 Tobacco & Trademark Claimants):** Successors and heirs of Menéndez, García y Cía. (CU-1335), Cifuentes y Cía. (CU-1336), F. Palicio y Cía., and other pre-1959 tobacco manufactory and vegas proprietors holding certified claims and final judgments.
2. **Tier 2 (Federal Terrorism & Wrongful Death Judgment Creditors):** Holders of final federal judgments against the Republic of Cuba and its commercial instrumentalities under 28 U.S.C. § 1605A and TRIA § 201.
3. **Tier 3 (Cuban Public Restorative Agro-Industrial Trust):** Allocation for modern agricultural revitalization, reforestation of damaged tobacco soils in Vuelta Abajo, independent farmer land-title restoration, and health clinic support for tobacco sector workers.
