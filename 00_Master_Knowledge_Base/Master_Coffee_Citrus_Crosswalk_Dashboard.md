## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Architecture

This Evidentiary Cross-Walk Dashboard anchors Campaign 34 of the Global Restitution Alliance post-judgment enforcement apparatus. It operationalizes a coordinated multi-theater offensive against **$270.00 Billion** in sovereign agricultural export monopolies, expropriated mountain coffee latifundia (Sierra Maestra, Escambray), industrial citrus mega-plantations (**Empresa Agroindustrial Victoria de Girón** in Jagüey Grande), prime tropical cocoa plantations (Baracoa), timber concessions, state agricultural combines (**Grupo Empresarial Agroforestal / GAF**, **CubaCafé**, **Empresa Procesadora de Café Asdrúbal López**), and global commodity export monopolies managed by **CubaExport** across Tokyo, Rotterdam, Hamburg, Geneva, and Milan.

Following the revolutionary confiscations of 1959-1960 under Law 851 and Law 890, the Cuban regime nationalized the nation's entire agricultural and agro-industrial sector without compensation. Confiscated properties included the extensive high-altitude arabica coffee estates of American and Cuban private planters in Oriente and Las Villas, thousands of hectares of prime citrus land in Matanzas and Jagüey Grande, prime cocoa fermentation estates in Baracoa, and American-owned timber and forestry reserves.

In modern times, the Cuban state commercialized these expropriated latifundia into hard-currency export monopolies operated under the Ministry of Agriculture (**MINAG**) and the **GAESA** military commercial apparatus:
1. **Specialty Gourmet Coffee Export Monopolies:** **Grupo Empresarial Agroforestal (GAF)** and **CubaCafé** (via the Asdrúbal López processing combine in Guantánamo) maintain a strict state monopoly over the processing and export of the island's high-grade arabica coffee crops (**Crystal Mountain**, **Turquino Montañés**, **Serrano Superior**). These coffees command premium auction prices ($8,000 – $12,000 per metric ton) in Japan (UCC Ueshima Coffee Co.), Germany, Italy (Lavazza / Illy suppliers), and France, with multi-million-dollar proceeds routed through European and Japanese correspondent clearing banks.
2. **Victoria de Girón Citrus Mega-Combine:** Encompassing over 50,000 hectares of expropriated land in Jagüey Grande, Matanzas, this industrial combine represents the largest citrus plantation in the Americas. Operating modern automated extraction and juice concentration facilities (historically partnered with foreign multinational investors such as the BM Group), it exports tens of thousands of metric tons of frozen concentrated orange juice (FCOJ), grapefruit concentrate, and high-value essential oils (D-Limonene) to European blending terminals in Rotterdam and Antwerp.
3. **Baracoa Industrial Cocoa Combines:** The **Empresa de Derivados del Cacao** in Baracoa extracts high-fat cocoa butter, cocoa liquor, and organic chocolate products for hard-currency commercial sale to European chocolatiers and domestic resort tourism hotels.
4. **CubaExport Foreign Trade Monopolies:** Serving as the exclusive state foreign trade enterprise routing export sales contracts, letters of credit, and foreign trade receivables through European and Asian banking rails.

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2) (commercial activity exception) and 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2) & § 1610(g) under *Bancec*, international agricultural commodity exports, auction settlements, and citrus juice blending sales constitute purely private commercial commerce (*jure gestionis*). Consequently, all foreign coffee auction receivables, citrus concentrate export earnings, cocoa butter trade disbursements, and commodity trade escrows are subject to immediate post-judgment garnishment and attachment across the United States, Japan, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Germany, and Spain.
