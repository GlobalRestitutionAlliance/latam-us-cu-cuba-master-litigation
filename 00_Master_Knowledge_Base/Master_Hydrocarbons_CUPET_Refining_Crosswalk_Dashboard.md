### Executive Summary & Jurisprudential Mandate

This Evidentiary Cross-Walk Dashboard anchors **Campaign 44** of the Global Restitution Alliance post-judgment enforcement apparatus. It operationalizes a coordinated multi-theater offensive against **$290.00 Billion** in sovereign hydrocarbon extraction combines, coastal oil refineries, offshore exploration concessions, transnational energy joint ventures, deepwater tanker terminal infrastructure, and hard-currency retail fuel distribution monopolies. Target institutions include the state petroleum holding corporation (**Unión Cuba-Petróleo - CUPET**), national coastal refineries (**Refinería Ñico López, Refinería Hermanos Díaz, Refinería Camilo Cienfuegos, Refinería Sergio Soto**), the **Matanzas Supertanker Terminal (Base de Supertanqueros)**, the **Matanzas-Cienfuegos crude pipeline**, joint ventures with foreign energy corporations (**Energas S.A. / Sherritt International, Cuvenpetrol S.A. / PDVSA, Zarubezhneft, Melbana Energy**), and the **Servicupet** nationwide hard-currency fuel retailing chain.

The expropriation of American petroleum assets in June–August 1960 marked the decisive breach of international law triggering the U.S. trade embargo. Acting under Resolution No. 1 and Law 851, the Cuban regime seized the monumental coastal refineries and distribution networks of **Standard Oil of New Jersey (Esso Belot Refinery)**, **Texaco (Santiago de Cuba Refinery)**, and **Sinclair Oil Corporation (Marine Terminals and Wholesale Distribution Depots)** after these companies lawfully declined to process Soviet crude oil. These expropriations were certified as compensable takings by the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) under Title V of the International Claims Settlement Act of 1949, representing some of the largest single certified awards in U.S. history.

Under **28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)** (Commercial Activity Sovereign Exception), **28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)** (Commercial Takings Exception), **28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2)**, and **28 U.S.C. § 1610(g)** under *Bancec*, CUPET and its operating subsidiaries operate as commercial instrumentalities and alter egos of the Ministry of Energy and Mines (MINEM) and the Cuban state.
