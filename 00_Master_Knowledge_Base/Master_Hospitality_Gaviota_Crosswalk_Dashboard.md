- **Instruments:** `HOSP-001` through `HOSP-005` (5 Instruments, $85.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) certified awards covering landmark nationalizations under Law 851 (July 6, 1960). Specifically, Claim No. CU-0249 (Hotel San Carlos in Cienfuegos, owned by the Mata family), Claim Nos. CU-1100 & CU-2495 (historic beachfront real estate and resort properties in Varadero Beach owned by the Echevarria and Trinidad families), and Claim Nos. CU-2500 & CU-2501 (archipelago cay resort latifundia across Cayo Coco, Cayo Santa María, and Playa Pesquero, Holguín). Confiscated assets also include Havana's crown-jewel pre-1959 hospitality landmarks: Havana Hilton (Habana Libre), Hotel Nacional de Cuba, and Habana Riviera.
- **Strategic Posture:** Establishes that current commercial resort operations are built upon stolen, uncompensated private real estate, providing the foundational jurisdictional nexus for in rem title claims, statutory trafficking actions under Helms-Burton Title III, and property restitution liens under international law.

### Wave 2: GAESA Military Conglomerate, Grupo de Turismo Gaviota S.A., Cubanacán & Gran Caribe Monopolies ($75.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `HOSP-006` through `HOSP-010` (5 Instruments, $75.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Corporate records, military decrees, and foreign commercial filings demonstrating that **Grupo de Turismo Gaviota S.A.** is an operational arm of the Cuban military's holding conglomerate, **GAESA**, directly controlled by MINFAR generals. Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec*, 462 U.S. 611 (1983) and *Rubin v. Islamic Republic of Iran*, 138 S. Ct. 816 (2018), Gaviota, Cubanacán S.A., and Gran Caribe S.A. function as commercial alter egos lacking independent juridical existence. Their commercial hotel revenues, hard-currency intake facilities, and engineering arms (EMPRESTUR S.A.) are fully subject to post-judgment execution under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2) and § 1610(g).

### Wave 3: Transnational Hotel Management Combines & Joint Venture Interdiction ($70.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `HOSP-011` through `HOSP-015` (5 Instruments, $70.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Bilateral hotel management agreements (HMAs) and joint venture contracts between Cuban state entities and foreign hospitality multinationals: **Meliá Hotels International S.A.** (operating over 30 hotels in Cuba), **Iberostar Hoteles y Apartamentos S.L.** (operating over 18 hotels), **Blue Diamond Resorts / Sunwing Travel Group** (operating over 20 resorts under Memories, Royalton, and Mystique brands), **Kempinski AG** (operating Gran Hotel Manzana Kempinski Havana), and **Accor S.A.** (operating SO/ Paseo del Prado).
- **Garnishment Vectors:** Base management fees (typically 2–3% of gross revenues), marketing contributions, and incentive fees (8–10% of gross operating profit) payable by Cuban resort entities to corporate headquarters in Spain, Canada, Switzerland, and France are commercial contractual debts garnishable via Rule 69 citations and European enforcement procedures.

### Wave 4: Helms-Burton Title III Trafficking Actions, GDS Booking Networks & Tour Operator Escrows ($50.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `HOSP-016` through `HOSP-020` (5 Instruments, $50.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Statutory civil liability under 22 U.S.C. § 6082 for trafficking in confiscated property. Certified claim holders are entitled to money damages equal to the certified amount plus compound interest, trebled under § 6082(a)(3). Furthermore, electronic booking clearinghouses—including **Amadeus IT Group S.A.** (Madrid/Miami), **Sabre Corporation** (Texas), and **Hotelbeds / HBX Group** (Mallorca/Florida)—and European/Canadian tour operators (**TUI AG**, **DER Touristik**, **Sunwing Travel Group**) hold millions of dollars in transit in centralized settlement accounts, which are subject to pre- and post-judgment garnishment writs.
