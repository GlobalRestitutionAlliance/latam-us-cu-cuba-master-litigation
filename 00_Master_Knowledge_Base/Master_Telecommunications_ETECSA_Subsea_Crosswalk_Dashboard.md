### Executive Summary & Jurisprudential Mandate

This Evidentiary Cross-Walk Dashboard anchors **Campaign 46** of the Global Restitution Alliance post-judgment enforcement apparatus. It operationalizes a coordinated multi-theater offensive against **$290.00 Billion** in sovereign telecommunications monopolies, expropriated pre-1959 American telephone utility latifundia, submarine fiber-optic cables, international IP transit corridors, multilateral mobile roaming clearing balances, and global hard-currency mobile phone recharge gateways. Target institutions include the state telecommunications monopoly (**Empresa de Telecomunicaciones de Cuba S.A. - ETECSA**), its mobile cellular operating division (**Cubacel**), broadband and WiFi networks (**Nauta**), submarine cable joint ventures (**Telecomunicaciones Gran Caribe - TGC / ALBA-1**), the **Arcos-1** cable landing branch at Cojímar, the **Jaruco Satellite Earth Station**, international roaming clearinghouses (**EDCH, Syniverse**), and digital mobile recharge aggregators (**Ding, Recharge.com, CSQ World, IDT / Boss Revolution**).

In August 1960 under Law 851 and Executive Resolution No. 1, the Cuban regime confiscated the nationwide assets of the **Cuban Telephone Company (Compañía Telefónica Cubana)**, an operating subsidiary of the **International Telephone & Telegraph Corporation (ITT)**. This expropriation was certified as compensable under Title V of the International Claims Settlement Act of 1949 (FCSC Claim No. CU-2615, Decision No. CU-5014), certifying a 1960 corporate principal value of **$130,647,000.00**—representing one of the most significant certified awards against Cuba, now worth tens of billions in restitution. The seized assets included the monumental ten-story central headquarters at Águila y Dragones in Havana, automated metropolitan step-by-step switching exchanges across every major city, nationwide rights-of-way, and transoceanic submarine telephone cables linking Cuba to the United States.

Under **28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)** (Commercial Activity Sovereign Exception), **28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)** (Commercial Takings Exception), **28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2)**, and **28 U.S.C. § 1610(g)** under *Bancec*, ETECSA operates as a purely commercial agency and alter ego of the Ministry of Communications (MINCOM) and the Cuban government. Commercial cellular networks, submarine fiber-optic cable capacity rights, international mobile roaming settlement receivables, and millions of monthly hard-currency mobile phone top-ups processed abroad constitute commercial property subject to post-judgment attachment under Rule 69 execution, third-party debtor garnishment, and permanent turnover into the Master Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF).
