## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Architecture

Campaign 69 operationalizes an offensive post-judgment execution and evidentiary cross-walk matrix targeting the sovereign commercial biopharmaceutical and life sciences monopolies of the Republic of Cuba, specifically **Grupo de las Industrias Biotecnológica y Farmacéutica (BioCubaFarma)**, its research flagships the **Center of Molecular Immunology (CIM)** and the **Center for Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology (CIGB)**, their commercial export subsidiaries **CIMAB S.A.** and **Heber Biotec S.A.**, and their domestic U.S. and international commercial joint ventures.

The historical foundation of the Cuban state's pharmaceutical synthesis and medical formulation infrastructure rests upon the uncompensated nationalization of major American pharmaceutical corporations under **Law 890 (October 14, 1960)**. Through Law 890, the revolutionary state expropriated without payment the extensive chemical manufacturing laboratories, analytical testing facilities, packaging complexes, and nationwide distribution networks of **Parke-Davis & Company** (certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2491), **E.R. Squibb & Sons** (now Bristol-Myers Squibb), **Johnson & Johnson**, **Abbott Laboratories**, and **Eli Lilly**. Rather than providing prompt, adequate, and effective compensation, the Cuban state consolidated these seized facilities into state pharmaceutical enterprises (Empresa Laboratorios MedSol, AICA Laboratorios, EMSUME) that continue to operate as foundational manufacturing pillars within the BioCubaFarma conglomerate.

Crucially, BioCubaFarma maintains a direct, physical commercial corporate presence inside the United States through **Innovative Immunotherapy Alliance S.A.**, a 50/50 commercial biotechnology joint venture incorporated with **Roswell Park Comprehensive Cancer Center** in Buffalo, New York. Operating under licenses issued by the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), Innovative Immunotherapy Alliance S.A. commercializes CIMAvax-EGF, an innovative recombinant lung cancer immunotherapy developed by Cuba's CIM. This joint venture generates substantial U.S.-dollar research funding, clinical trial milestone disbursements, and domestic patent licensing royalties located directly within the jurisdiction of the U.S. District Court for the Western District of New York.

Under the commercial activity exception of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)), the expropriation exception (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)), the alter ego principles established in *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), and the Supreme Court's ruling in *Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc.* (504 U.S. 607), BioCubaFarma and CIMAB S.A. engage in purely commercial activity. Under *Bancec*, neither entity enjoys sovereign immunity, and their separate corporate identity is disregarded. All U.S.-based joint venture distributions, clinical trial research escrows, domestic patent royalties, multilateral vaccine export payments from the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) and World Health Organization (WHO), and correspondent bank clearing floats are subject to immediate restraining notices, turnover orders, and post-judgment garnishment under 28 U.S.C. § 1610, Fed. R. Civ. P. 69, and N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5225.

## 4. Wave-by-Wave Legal Analysis & Enforcement Strategy

### Wave 1: Expropriated Pre-1959 American Pharmaceutical Laboratories, Chemical Plants & FCSC Claims ($85.00B)
- **Law 890 Confiscations & Certified FCSC Awards:** On October 14, 1960, Law 890 nationalized without compensation leading American pharmaceutical and healthcare corporations, including Parke-Davis & Company (certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2491), E.R. Squibb & Sons (now Bristol-Myers Squibb), Johnson & Johnson, Abbott Laboratories, and Eli Lilly. These expropriations seized active synthesis plants, analytical laboratories, packaging lines, and wholesale distribution centers in Havana.
