## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Architecture

Campaign 67 establishes an exhaustive evidentiary cross-walk matrix targeting the sovereign commercial brewing and malt beverage monopolies of the Republic of Cuba, specifically **Cervecería Bucanero S.A.**, operating as a 50/50 transnational joint venture between the Cuban state holding company **Corporación Alimentaria S.A. (Coralsa)** and the world's largest multinational brewing conglomerate, **Anheuser-Busch InBev (AB InBev)**.

The historical and property title predicate of the entire Cuban commercial brewing industry rests upon the uncompensated mass confiscations executed under **Law 890 (October 14, 1960)**. Through Law 890, the Cuban state nationalized Cuba's historic brewing empires: **Cervecería Polar S.A.** (Havana), **Cervecería La Tropical** (Nueva Fábrica de Hielo S.A.), and Bacardi's **Cervecería Hatuey** (with major industrial plants in Santiago de Cuba, Caimito, and Manacas). The Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) formally adjudicated and certified final awards for these confiscated brewing complexes, bottling lines, and trademark assets. Rather than compensating the owners, the Cuban state seized these production and distribution facilities, later consolidating modern production into the massive **Holguín Mega-Brewery** operated by Cervecería Bucanero S.A.

Today, Cervecería Bucanero S.A. holds an absolute, non-competitive commercial monopoly over the production, wholesale supply, and retail sale of beer across Cuba. Its flagship brands—**Cristal** and **Bucanero**—monopolize all beverage sales across GAESA tourist hotel conglomerates (Gaviota, Gran Caribe, Cubanacán) and hard-currency retail networks (**Tiendas MLC**). This generates massive, recurring hard-currency profits that are distributed as corporate dividends and management fees to AB InBev and state holding entities through European and Canadian banking rails. Furthermore, Bucanero imports and distributes foreign premium brands (Stella Artois, Beck's, Budweiser) and procures hundreds of millions in imported barley, malt, and hops via European factoring brokers.

Under the commercial activity exception of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)), the expropriation exception (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)), the alter ego doctrine of *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), and Title III of the Helms-Burton Act (22 U.S.C. § 6082), neither Bucanero nor Coralsa enjoy sovereign immunity. Multinational participants trafficking in confiscated brewery assets are subject to statutory treble damages, while joint venture dividend escrows, trade factoring balances, and correspondent bank accounts are fully reachable for post-judgment garnishment and turnover under 28 U.S.C. § 1610, Fed. R. Civ. P. 69, and N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5225.

## 4. Wave-by-Wave Legal Analysis & Enforcement Strategy

### Wave 1: Expropriated Pre-1959 American & Cuban Breweries, Trademarks & FCSC Claims ($85.00B)
- **Law 890 Confiscations & Certified Awards:** Enacted on October 14, 1960, Law 890 nationalized without compensation Cuba's major private breweries, including Cervecería Polar S.A., Cervecería La Tropical, and Cervecería Hatuey. These nationalizations included extensive fermentation tanks, bottling plants, industrial water wells, distribution fleets, and world-famous beer trademarks.
- **Helms-Burton Title III Trafficking Actions:** The commercial use and exploitation of confiscated brewing facilities, deep-well water sources, and historical trademarks by state monopolies and foreign joint venture partners constitutes actionable trafficking under 22 U.S.C. § 6082, creating liability for statutory treble damages against commercial operators.

### Wave 2: Cervecería Bucanero S.A. Sovereign Monopoly & Industrial Infrastructure ($75.00B)
- **Commercial Beer Monopoly:** Operating the ultra-modern Holguín Mega-Brewery, Cervecería Bucanero S.A. holds an absolute commercial monopoly over domestic beer production (Cristal, Bucanero). It supplies 100% of the beer consumed across GAESA tourist resort hotels and Tiendas MLC retail networks.
- **Commercial Disgorgement:** Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), Bucanero and Coralsa are commercial alter egos of the Cuban state. Their domestic wholesale revenues, hotel supply concessions, and retail cash sweeps are non-immune commercial funds subject to post-judgment turnover under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2).

### Wave 3: Anheuser-Busch InBev (AB InBev) Joint Venture, Factoring & Title III Trafficking ($75.00B)
