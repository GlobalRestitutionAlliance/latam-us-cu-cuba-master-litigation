### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & ARCHITECTURAL FOUNDATION

Campaign 84 enforces $430.00 Billion in sovereign and transnational commercial liabilities across the Republic of Cuba, its state consumer chemical monopoly **Empresa Suchel** (operating under the **Grupo Empresarial de la Industria Química - GEIQ** and the Ministry of Industries - MINDUS), the state joint-venture holding vehicle **Intersuchel S.A.**, transnational consumer conglomerate **Unilever PLC** (London / Rotterdam), Spanish cosmetics partner **Perfumería Camacho** (Suchel Camacho S.A.), Vietnamese industrial partner **Thai Binh Corporation** (Suchel TBV S.A.), and foreign chemical supply rails.

The campaign systematically remediates the uncompensated confiscation of Cuba's pre-1959 domestic and multinational consumer chemical, soap, synthetic detergent, and personal care industries:

1. **Expropriated Pre-1959 Manufacturing Latifundia:** Nationalized under Law 851 of July 6, 1960 and Law 890 of October 13, 1960, encompassing the master production complexes of **Colgate-Palmolive (Cuba) S.A.** (FCSC Claim No. CU-0730, Decision No. CU-4547; continuous saponification towers, detergent spray-drying plants, and toothpaste lines in Luyanó and Marianao); **The Procter & Gamble Company / Sabatés Industrial S.A.** (FCSC Claim Nos. CU-0569 & CU-0568; edible oil processing, hydrogenated fats, and synthetic detergent plants in Regla and Puentes Grandes producing *Tide*, *Ace*, and *Jabón Llave*); and the historic nationalized perfume and soap manufacturer **Crusellas y Compañía S.A.** All claims are authenticated through National Archives records (NARA RG 234) and indexed with 66 years of 6.0% simple annual interest pursuant to Title V of the International Claims Settlement Act of 1949 (22 U.S.C. § 1643l).
2. **Empresa Suchel State Monopolies:** Reorganized following the 1960 confiscations under MINBAS/MINDUS and GEIQ, Empresa Suchel exercises an absolute domestic monopoly over soap, synthetic detergent, personal hygiene, and household chemical production. Suchel controls historic facilities in Luyanó and Regla, distributing consumer goods nationwide through state commercial retail chains (**Corporación CIMEX S.A.** and **Tiendas Caribe / TRD**) at exorbitant hard-currency markups in Tiendas MLC.
3. **Unilever Suchel S.A. & Mariel Special Development Zone (ZEDM) Concessions:** In 2016, multinational conglomerate Unilever PLC formed a 60/40 joint venture with the Cuban state (Intersuchel S.A.), investing over $35 million in a 40,000 square meter industrial complex in Sector A of the Mariel Special Development Zone. The venture manufactures and distributes global Unilever brands (*Sedal*, *Rexona*, *Close Up*, *Lux*, *Omo*, *Surf*) to substitute consumer imports, exploiting confiscated state infrastructure and utilizing state-directed labor through employment agencies (**ASETUR / CUBATECNICA**) that skim 90–98% of workers' earned compensation.
4. **Helms-Burton Act Title III & IV Corporate Actions:** Direct statutory cause of action against Unilever PLC (London) and Spanish partner Camacho under 22 U.S.C. § 6082 for knowingly and intentionally trafficking in confiscated chemical manufacturing real estate, industrial infrastructure, and brand distribution channels. Pleading fee-simple ownership across confiscated industrial tracts immunizes plaintiffs from term-concession expiration defenses (*Havana Docks Corp. v. Royal Caribbean Cruises, Ltd.*, 104 F.4th 1243), triggering automatic treble damages under 22 U.S.C. § 6082(a)(3) and mandatory executive visa revocations under 22 U.S.C. § 6091.
5. **Transnational Parent Company Duty of Care & European Tort Liability:** Under the landmark UK Supreme Court precedent in *Vedanta Resources Plc v. Lungowe* [2019] UKSC 20 and *Okpabi v. Royal Dutch Shell Plc* [2021] UKSC 3, Unilever PLC owes an affirmative common-law duty of care for the joint venture operations of Unilever Suchel S.A. in Cuba. Concurrent claims under Dutch Civil Code Book 6 Art. 162 (*onrechtmatige daad*) and EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) establish parent company tort liability for complicity in property deprivation and wage skimming.
6. **Cross-Border Enforcement & Master Qualified Settlement Fund (26 U.S.C. § 468B):** Deploying garnishment petitions against Unilever Level III American Depositary Receipts (ADRs: NYSE UL), U.S. trademark royalties, and commercial receivables; serving European Account Preservation Orders (Regulation (EU) No 655/2014) across banks in London, Amsterdam, and Madrid; and depositing all liquidated recoveries directly into a court-supervised 26 U.S.C. § 468B Master QSF Trust.
