## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Legal Architecture
Campaign 62 operationalizes an offensive multi-jurisdictional post-judgment execution campaign targeting **$340.00 Billion** in sovereign commercial hydrocarbon refining monopolies, deepwater supertanker terminals, offshore exploration production-sharing concessions, and transnational crude tanker fleet bunkering escrows. Under the command of the Omni-Counsel Architect, this campaign targets the state commercial energy combines of Cuba: **Unión Cuba-Petróleo (CUPET)**, wholesale fuel monopoly **Empresa Comercializadora de Combustibles (ECC)**, upstream producer **Empresa de Perforación y Extracción de Petróleo (EPEP)**, and retail commercial fuel marketer **Corporación CIMEX S.A.** (Servicupet / Oro Negro).

The campaign is anchored in four formidable statutory and judicial pillars:
1. **Expropriated American Refineries & FCSC Certified Claims (CU-0064 & CU-0065):** On July 6, 1960, the Cuban Revolutionary Government enacted Law 851, nationalizing American oil refineries without compensation. In 1969, the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) certified Claim No. CU-0064 in favor of **Esso Standard Oil S.A.** (now ExxonMobil Corp.) for $71,611,002.90, and Claim No. CU-0065 in favor of **Texaco Inc.** (now Chevron Corp.) for $50,081,109.70. With accrued interest, these certified claims represent multi-billion-dollar liabilities establishing an unassailable in rem property nexus under FSIA § 1605(a)(3) and 22 U.S.C. § 6082.
2. **D.D.C. Helms-Burton Title III Precedent (*Exxon Mobil Corp. v. CIMEX & CUPET*):** In *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Corporación CIMEX S.A. and Unión Cuba-Petróleo*, Civil Action No. 19-1277 (D.D.C.), the federal court upheld subject-matter jurisdiction under Helms-Burton Title III and FSIA commercial exceptions, affirming that CUPET's and CIMEX's commercial exploitation of confiscated refinery assets constitutes actionable 'trafficking' subject to treble damages.
3. **Matanzas Deepwater Supertanker Terminal & Strategic Crude Storage:** The supertanker terminal at Bahía de Matanzas is Cuba's sole deepwater terminal capable of handling 150,000+ DWT crude supertankers (VLCCs), featuring 2.4+ million barrels of storage capacity. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2) and § 1610(a)(2), the terminal's commercial throughput fees, storage receipts, and berthing dues are non-immune assets subject to turnover orders.
4. **Transnational Tanker Fleets, Bunkering Escrows & Commodity Swaps:** Crude oil tanker fleets operated or chartered by Cuban state entities (Empresa Santander Navegación S.A.) entering foreign territorial waters are subject to Supplemental Admiralty Rule B attachments and Rule C in rem arrests. Furthermore, international oil trading escrows (Trafigura, Glencore, Vitol) and foreign joint venture production-sharing escrows (Sherritt International Block 10) are garnishable under Rule 69.

## 3. Wave-by-Wave Forensic Analysis & Recovery Vectors

### Wave 1: Expropriated Pre-1959 American Oil Refineries, Law 851 Confiscations & Certified FCSC Claims ($90.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `PETR-001` through `PETR-005` (5 Instruments, $90.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Certified FCSC claims establishing the uncompensated confiscation of American and foreign oil refineries under Law 851 (July 6, 1960). In Claim No. CU-0064, Esso Standard Oil S.A. (ExxonMobil) was awarded $71.6M for its Belot refinery in Havana Bay, terminals, and service stations. In Claim No. CU-0065, Texaco Inc. was awarded $50.1M for its refinery, bulk storage depots, and piers. Compañía Petrolera Shell de Cuba and Sinclair Cuban Petroleum assets were likewise absorbed into the state monopoly without compensation.
- **Strategic Posture:** Establishes that the modern Cuban petroleum refining and storage infrastructure was built directly upon uncompensated expropriated American private property, satisfying FSIA § 1605(a)(3) and Helms-Burton Title III (22 U.S.C. § 6082).

### Wave 2: Unión Cuba-Petróleo (CUPET), ECC & Sovereign Petroleum Refining Monopolies ($75.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `PETR-006` through `PETR-010` (5 Instruments, $75.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** State decrees and corporate registries proving that **Unión Cuba-Petróleo (CUPET)** and wholesale distributor **Empresa Comercializadora de Combustibles (ECC)** operate as commercial instrumentalities under the Ministry of Energy and Mines (MINEM) and GAESA. Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec*, 462 U.S. 611 (1983) and *Rubin v. Islamic Republic of Iran*, 138 S. Ct. 816 (2018), CUPET and ECC lack separate juridical existence. Their commercial assets—including Refinería Camilo Cienfuegos, Refinería Ñico López, and Refinería Hermanos Díaz—are commercial property subject to post-judgment execution under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2) and § 1610(g).

### Wave 3: Matanzas Supertanker Terminal, Offshore Joint Ventures & Commodity Oil Swaps ($75.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `PETR-011` through `PETR-015` (5 Instruments, $75.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Infrastructure assets and commercial joint ventures centering on the deepwater supertanker terminal at Bahía de Matanzas (2.4M-barrel tank farm, deepwater VLCC jetties). Production-sharing contracts (PSCs) executed with Canada's **Sherritt International Corp.** (Block 10 / Puerto Escondido) and enhanced oil recovery joint ventures with Russia's **Zarubezhneft JSC** (Boca de Jaruco), alongside Venezuelan (PDVSA) and Russian (Rosneft) crude shipment swap escrows, provide direct targets for post-judgment garnishment.

### Wave 4: Helms-Burton Title III Trafficking Actions, Commercial Retail Fuel & Tanker Bunkering ($50.00 Billion Pool)
- **Instruments:** `PETR-016` through `PETR-020` (5 Instruments, $50.00B Total).
- **Evidentiary Anchors:** Active federal court precedents in *Exxon Mobil Corp. v. CIMEX & CUPET*, Civil Action No. 19-1277 (D.D.C.), establishing statutory treble damages trafficking liabilities. Retail service station networks (Servicupet and CIMEX Oro Negro) selling fuel exclusively in hard currency (USD/MLC) generate commercial cash flows garnishable via Rule 69 turnover decrees. Concurrently, international marine bunkering clearing accounts settled in Rotterdam, Gibraltar, and Panama, chartered product tankers (Empresa Santander Navegación S.A.), and commodity trading escrows (Trafigura, Glencore, Vitol) are targeted through Supplemental Admiralty Rule B attachments.

### Wave 5: Alter Ego Determination, Energy Receivership & Master QSF Distribution ($50.00 Billion Pool)
