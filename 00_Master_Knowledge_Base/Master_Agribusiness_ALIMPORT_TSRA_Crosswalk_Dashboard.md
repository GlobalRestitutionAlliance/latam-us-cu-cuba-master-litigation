## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Architecture

Campaign 68 establishes an exhaustive evidentiary cross-walk matrix targeting the sovereign commercial food and agricultural import monopolies of the Republic of Cuba, specifically **Empresa Cubana Importadora de Alimentos (ALIMPORT)**, operating under the Ministry of Foreign Trade (MINCEX) and Corporación Alimentaria S.A. (Coralsa), and its multi-hundred-million-dollar annual trade finance conduits authorized under the **Trade Sanctions Reform and Export Enhancement Act of 2000 (TSRA)**.

The historical and property title predicate of the state's centralized food processing and grain storage infrastructure is rooted in the mass confiscations of American and Cuban agro-industrial giants under **Law 890 (October 14, 1960)**. Through Law 890, the Cuban state seized without compensation the extensive grain processing mills and cereal plants of **The Quaker Oats Company** (certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2497), the monumental flour mills, pneumatic elevators, and deepwater bulk grain discharge wharves of **Molinera de Harina S.A.** in Havana Bay, and the extensive industrial meatpacking and cold-storage networks of **Swift & Company** and **Armour & Company**. Rather than compensating these American corporations, the state incorporated their physical mills, grain elevators, and distribution warehouses directly into ALIMPORT's nationwide logistics network.

Under the statutory framework of TSRA (22 U.S.C. § 7207 et seq.), Cuba is permitted to purchase agricultural commodities (poultry, corn, soybeans, wheat, pork, rice) from major American agribusiness corporations (Cargill, Archer Daniels Midland, Tyson Foods, Bunge, Perdue Farms). However, TSRA strictly mandates that all sales be executed on a **"cash-in-advance"** basis or through third-party non-U.S. bank financing. Consequently, ALIMPORT continuously maintains tens of millions of dollars in liquid cash collateral escrows, pre-funded deposits, and documentary letters of credit at intermediary banks in Europe (BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole, BBVA, Banco Sabadell), Canada, Panama, and U.S. confirming banks before vessels depart Gulf ports.

Under the commercial activity exception of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)), the expropriation exception (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)), the alter ego doctrine of *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), and the landmark Supreme Court ruling in *Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc.* (504 U.S. 607), ALIMPORT's agricultural commodity procurement is purely commercial activity. Neither ALIMPORT nor MINCEX enjoy sovereign immunity. Their commercial cash collateral deposits, TSRA escrow floats, letters of credit, in-transit agricultural cargo, and correspondent accounts are subject to post-judgment garnishment, in rem maritime arrest, and turnover under 28 U.S.C. § 1610, Supplemental Admiralty Rule C, Fed. R. Civ. P. 69, and N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5225.

## 4. Wave-by-Wave Legal Analysis & Enforcement Strategy

### Wave 1: Expropriated Pre-1959 American Food & Milling Plants, Silos & FCSC Claims ($85.00B)
- **Law 890 Confiscations & Certified FCSC Awards:** On October 14, 1960, Law 890 nationalized without compensation major American food corporations, including The Quaker Oats Company (certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2497), Molinera de Harina S.A. (Havana flour mills and grain elevators), Swift & Company, and Armour & Company. These expropriations encompassed industrial flour mills, pneumatic grain elevators, deepwater discharge berths in Regla, and nationwide cold-storage facilities.
- **Helms-Burton Title III Trafficking:** Commercial exploitation of these confiscated processing facilities, grain silos, and historical trademarks by state monopolies and foreign agricultural traders constitutes actionable trafficking under 22 U.S.C. § 6082, supporting statutory treble damages and constructive trusts over commercial food import revenues.

### Wave 2: ALIMPORT Sovereign Foreign Trade Monopoly & Agribusiness Procurement ($75.00B)
- **State Food Import Monopoly:** ALIMPORT holds an absolute state monopoly over all national commercial food purchasing, bulk grain procurement tenders, and wholesale distribution. ALIMPORT captures massive commercial margins supplying GAESA tourist hotels (ITH S.A.) and stocking hard-currency Tiendas MLC supermarkets across Cuba.
- **Commercial Disgorgement:** Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), ALIMPORT is an alter ego of the Cuban state. Its commercial food revenues, wholesale import margins, and port grain elevator concessions are non-immune commercial assets subject to turnover orders under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2).

### Wave 3: TSRA Cash-in-Advance Escrows & U.S. Agribusiness Trade Payables ($75.00B)
- **TSRA Cash Collateral Escrows:** Under 22 U.S.C. § 7207, Cuba's purchases from American agricultural suppliers (Cargill, ADM, Tyson, Bunge) must be paid via cash in advance. ALIMPORT maintains liquid cash deposits at confirming and intermediary banks to collateralize these purchases. Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 69 and N.Y.
