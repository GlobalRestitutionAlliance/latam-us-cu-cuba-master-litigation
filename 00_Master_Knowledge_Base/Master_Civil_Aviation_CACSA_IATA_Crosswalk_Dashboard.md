### Executive Summary & Jurisprudential Mandate

This Evidentiary Cross-Walk Dashboard anchors **Campaign 43** of the Global Restitution Alliance post-judgment enforcement apparatus. It operationalizes a coordinated multi-theater offensive against **$285.00 Billion** in sovereign commercial civil aviation holding monopolies, national flag carrier aircraft fleets, airport commercial concessions, en-route airspace navigation charges, and global ticket clearinghouse balances. Target institutions include the state civil aviation holding corporation (**Corporación de la Aviación Cubana S.A. - CACSA**), state flag carrier (**Empresa Cubana de Aviación S.A. - Cubana de Aviación**), GAESA's military-commercial air carrier (**Aerogaviota S.A.**), national airport authority (**Empresa Cubana de Aeropuertos y Servicios Aeroportuarios S.A. - ECASA**), air navigation monopoly (**Empresa Cubana de Navegación Aérea - ECNA**), the **IATA Clearing House (ICH)** in Geneva and Montreal, and global **Billing and Settlement Plan (BSP)** passenger remittance accounts across international commercial banking rails.

Following the revolutionary decrees of 1959-1960, the Cuban regime confiscated Cuba's civil aviation industry without compensation. Nationalized assets included **Compañía Cubana de Aviación S.A.** (historically developed in partnership with Pan American World Airways and operating Lockheed Super Constellations and Vickers Viscounts), private domestic air carriers **Aerovías Q S.A.** and **Cuba Aeropostal**, historic passenger terminals and maintenance hangars at Rancho Boyeros (now José Martí International Airport), and municipal aerodromes across the island. The regime consolidated all commercial aviation under state control, eventually creating CACSA to manage the flag carrier, airport infrastructure, air traffic navigation services, and duty-free airside retail.

Under **28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)** (Commercial Activity Sovereign Exception), **28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)** (Commercial Takings Exception), **28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2)**, and **28 U.S.C. § 1610(g)** under *Bancec*, CACSA, Cubana de Aviación, Aerogaviota, ECASA, and ECNA operate as purely commercial agencies and alter egos of the Cuban government. Commercial airline assets, passenger departure taxes embedded in international tickets, landing and parking fees owed by foreign carriers, en-route overflight fees paid by international airlines traversing the Havana Flight Information Region (FIR), and multilateral ticket settlements cleared via the IATA Clearing House constitute commercial property subject to post-judgment attachment, Rule 69 execution, and direct turnover into the Master Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF).
