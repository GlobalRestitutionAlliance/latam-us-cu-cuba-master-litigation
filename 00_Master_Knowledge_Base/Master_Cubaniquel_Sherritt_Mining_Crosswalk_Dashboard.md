### Strategic Mandate & Legal Foundations

Cuba possesses the world's third-largest reserves of lateritic nickel and globally significant deposits of strategic cobalt, centered in the Holguín Province (Moa Bay and Nicaro mining districts). These strategic mineral assets were expropriated without compensation under Law 851 from American corporations, prominently the **Freeport Sulphur Company / Moa Bay Mining Company** (certified by the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission under Claim No. CU-2498 for $88,349,000 principal in 1969, now exceeding $15 Billion with statutory interest) and the **Nicaro Nickel Project** (constructed and owned directly by the U.S. Government's Defense Plant Corporation during WWII and Korean War emergency stockpiling programs).

Today, these expropriated mineral latifundia are commercially exploited through the state monopoly **Grupo Empresarial Cubaníquel** and its transnational joint venture **Moa Nickel S.A.** (a 50/50 partnership between Empresa Central de Níquel and Canadian publicly traded entity **Sherritt International Corporation**, TSX: S). The mined mixed sulfides are transported by sea from Moa Bay to Sherritt's hydrometallurgical refinery in **Fort Saskatchewan, Alberta, Canada**, and the refined nickel and cobalt briquettes/cathodes are sold globally into international supply chains through offshore marketing conduit **International Cobalt Company Inc. (ICCI)** (Bahamas/Barbados), major off-take buyers including **China Minmetals Corporation**, European electric vehicle battery chemical manufacturers, and London Metal Exchange (LME) bonded warehouses.

This Campaign deploys comprehensive post-judgment remedies under:
1. **Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA) Commercial Activity Exceptions (28 U.S.C. §§ 1605(a)(2), 1610(a)(2)):** The extraction, processing, maritime export, and global sale of nickel and cobalt constitute non-immune commercial activities carried on outside the United States with substantial commercial effects within U.S. jurisdiction and commerce.
2. **First National City Bank v. Bancec (462 U.S. 611) Alter Ego Unity:** Grupo Empresarial Cubaníquel and Empresa Central de Níquel are fully dominated instrumentalities of the Council of Ministers and the Ministry of Energy and Mines (MINEM), allowing piercing of the corporate veil to satisfy judgments against the Republic of Cuba.
3. **Helms-Burton Act Title III Private Rights of Action (22 U.S.C. § 6082):** Any entity that commercializes, extracts, processes, refines, transports, buys, or facilitates commercial transactions involving minerals extracted from confiscated property "traffics" in such property and is subject to statutory treble damages in U.S. federal district courts.
4. **Helms-Burton Act Title IV Mandatory Sanctions (22 U.S.C. § 6091):** Corporate officers, directors, and controlling shareholders of multinational mining combines, battery manufacturers, and trading entities commercializing Moa Bay minerals are subject to mandatory U.S. entry exclusion and visa revocations.
5. **Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA § 201) & 28 U.S.C. § 1610(g):** Judgment creditors holding certified terror judgments against the Republic of Cuba may execute against all blocked and unblocked assets of Cuban sovereign agencies and instrumentalities, including Sherritt joint venture dividend escrows, debt repayment streams, and bank letters of credit.
6. **26 U.S.C. § 468B Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF) Trust Distribution:** All attached funds, seized mineral off-take accounts, and negotiated disgorgement settlements are deposited directly into a Delaware statutory fiduciary trust for pro-rata distribution to certified claimants and victims.
