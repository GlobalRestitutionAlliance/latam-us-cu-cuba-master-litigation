## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Architecture

Campaign 66 establishes an exhaustive evidentiary cross-walk matrix targeting the sovereign commercial national railway infrastructure of the Republic of Cuba, specifically **Unión de Ferrocarriles de Cuba (UFC)**, operating under the Ministry of Transport (MITRANS), and its multi-billion-dollar foreign equipment procurement, industrial freight, and intermodal transport concessions.

The foundational in rem and property title predicate of the entire 4,000+ mile Cuban national railway mainline rests directly upon the mass, uncompensated expropriations executed under **Law 890 (October 14, 1960)**. Through Law 890, the Cuban state seized the vast private railway network of **Consolidated Railroads of Cuba**—an American and Cuban corporation encompassing The Cuba Railroad Company, The Cuba Northern Railways Co., and associated terminals, certified by the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) under **Claim No. CU-2500**—as well as the electrified mainline, substations, and rolling stock of the **Hershey Cuban Railway** (certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2495). The state converted these private assets into the national rail network, continuing to exploit them commercially for industrial bulk sugar transport, container movements from Mariel Megaport, and nickel ore haulage.

In modern operations, UFC functions as a commercial enterprise that contracts with major foreign rolling stock manufacturers. Russian industrial conglomerates **Sinara Transport Machines** and **Transmashholding** have supplied dozens of new TGM8KM diesel locomotives, spare parts, and workshop modernizations under multi-hundred-million-euro contracts backed by the Russian Export Insurance Agency (**EXIAR**). Simultaneously, China's **CRRC Corporation Limited** has supplied hundreds of passenger rail coaches, diesel multiple units (DMUs), and freight wagons financed through multilateral commercial letters of credit and international banking conduits.

Under the commercial activity exception of the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2)), the expropriation exception (28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(3)), the alter ego doctrine of *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), and Title III of the Helms-Burton Act (22 U.S.C. § 6082), UFC does not enjoy sovereign immunity for its commercial freight operations. Its commercial assets, export credit escrows, foreign factoring balances, in-transit rolling stock shipments, and correspondent bank accounts are subject to post-judgment execution, in rem maritime arrest, garnishment, and turnover under 28 U.S.C. § 1610, Supplemental Admiralty Rule C, Fed. R. Civ. P. 69, and N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5225.

## 4. Wave-by-Wave Legal Analysis & Enforcement Strategy

### Wave 1: Expropriated Consolidated Railroads of Cuba, Hershey Electric Line & FCSC Claims ($85.00B)
- **Law 890 & FCSC Certified Claims:** On October 14, 1960, Law 890 confiscated without compensation the entirety of Consolidated Railroads of Cuba (certified under FCSC Claim No. CU-2500) and the electrified Hershey Cuban Railway (FCSC Claim No. CU-2495). This included over 4,000 miles of mainline trackage, switching yards, roundhouses, telegraph rights-of-way, and the iconic Havana Central Railway Terminal (Estación Central).
- **Helms-Burton Title III Trafficking Actions:** The ongoing commercial use of these confiscated tracks, bridges, and passenger terminals by UFC and foreign commercial shipping lines constitutes actionable trafficking in confiscated property under 22 U.S.C. § 6082, creating liability for statutory treble damages against commercial users and operators.

### Wave 2: Unión de Ferrocarriles de Cuba (UFC) Sovereign Monopoly & Industrial Tariffs ($75.00B)
- **Commercial Rail Freight Monopoly:** Unión de Ferrocarriles de Cuba (UFC) is a state commercial corporation under MITRANS exercising a total monopoly over industrial bulk freight transport. Key revenue streams include raw sugar transport contracts with AZCUBA centrales, container flatcar shuttles between Mariel Deepwater Megaport and inland distribution hubs, and nickel ore haulage in Holguín.
- **Commercial Disgorgement:** Under *First National City Bank v. Bancec* (462 U.S. 611), UFC is an alter ego of the Cuban state. Its commercial freight receivables and transport tariffs are non-immune assets subject to turnover orders under 28 U.S.C. § 1610(a)(2).

### Wave 3: Transnational Locomotive Supply Concessions & Foreign Factoring Escrows ($70.00B)
- **Russian & Chinese Supply Conduits:** UFC has acquired major fleets of modern diesel locomotives (Russian Sinara TGM8KM) and passenger/freight cars (Chinese CRRC). These procurements involve multi-million-dollar letters of credit, export credit guarantees from Russia's EXIAR, and intermediary bank payment floats.
- **In Rem Admiralty Arrest:** Locomotive shipments, traction motors, and wheelsets transiting Caribbean transshipment hubs (Panama, Bahamas, Dominican Republic) are subject to in rem arrest warrants under Supplemental Admiralty Rule C and international arrest conventions.

### Wave 4: Passenger Rail Tariffs, Rolling Stock Leasing & Central Clearing Conduits ($65.00B)
- **Passenger Fares & Interline Leasing:** UFC operates long-distance express passenger rail lines (including the Tren Francés) and interurban services. Foreign railcar leasing accounts, container demurrage escrows, and intermodal cargo receivables are reachable through post-judgment restraining notices served on international forwarders and clearing banks under N.Y. C.P.L.R. § 5222 and Fed. R. Civ. P. 69.

### Wave 5: Alter Ego Turnover, Factoring & Master QSF Restitution ($45.00B)
- **Turnover & Receivership:** Combining federal court turnover decrees with international exequatur proceedings, this wave attaches bank deposits in BFI, Havin Bank London, and BICSA, garnishes foreign supplier obligations, and appoints a federal equity receiver under Fed. R. Civ. P. 66 over UFC commercial freight revenue streams.
- **Master QSF Liquidation:** Disgorged funds are transferred directly into the Master Qualified Settlement Fund under 26 U.S.C. § 468B, providing pro rata restitution distributions to certified American railroad claimants (Consolidated Railroads and Hershey successors).
