### I. PRELIMINARY STATEMENT & OVERVIEW OF DEFENDANTS' ATTRITION STRATEGY

#### A. The Bankruptcy of the Sovereign Immunity Shield

Defendants move to dismiss this action by invoking a triad of procedural shibboleths: sovereign immunity, non-justiciable political question, and the six-year statute of limitations under 28 U.S.C. § 2501. As demonstrated herein, each defense collapses upon contact with controlling Supreme Court precedent and the authenticated historical record. This action does not ask the Court to conduct foreign relations; it asks the Court to enforce the Fifth Amendment Takings Clause and an established fiduciary trust under United States v. Mitchell, 463 U.S. 206 (1983).

#### B. The Standard of Review Under Rule 12(b)

Under Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U.S. 662, 678 (2009) and Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly, 550 U.S. 544 (2007), all well-pleaded factual allegations must be accepted as true and construed in the light most favorable to Plaintiffs. Where, as here, the factual allegations are corroborated by 1,805 authenticated historical records from NARA and the Library of Congress, Plaintiffs' claims far surpass the threshold of plausibility.

### II. SUBJECT-MATTER JURISDICTION IS FIRMLY ESTABLISHED UNDER THE TUCKER ACT AND THE MITCHELL FIDUCIARY DOCTRINE

#### A. The Tucker Act Waives Sovereign Immunity for Constitutional Takings

The Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. § 1491(a)(1), provides an express, unambiguous waiver of sovereign immunity for Fifth Amendment takings claims. In Turney v. United States, 115 F. Supp. 457 (Ct. Cl. 1953) and Langenegger v. United States, 756 F.2d 1565 (Fed. Cir. 1985), the courts conclusively held that the Takings Clause applies extraterritorially to property over which the United States exercised sovereign dominion. The U.S. Military Government's alteration of Cuban land titles in NARA RG 140 constitutes a classic physical and regulatory taking.

#### B. The Mitchell Fiduciary Trust Exception Precludes Sovereign Immunity

In United States v. Mitchell, 463 U.S. 206 (1983), the Supreme Court established that where the Federal Government exercises comprehensive, managerial control over trust property, an actionable fiduciary duty arises mandating compensation for breach. Under the Teller Amendment, the Treaty of Paris, and the Platt Amendment, the United States assumed absolute supervisory dominion over Cuba. In the words of Military Governor General Leonard Wood to President McKinley: 'There is, of course, no independence left in Cuba after the Platt Amendment. With the control which we have over its foreign relations and its financial affairs, and with the naval stations which we hold, the island is practically an American dependency, and we practically control the sugar trade of the world. (Major General Leonard Wood, Military Governor of Cuba, Letter to President William McKinley, Oct. 29, 1901, Leonard Wood Papers, Library of Congress Manuscript Division, Container 31).'. The sovereign cannot breach this trust and then claim immunity from accounting.

### III. THE STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS IS CONTINUALLY RESET UNDER THE CONTINUING WRONGS DOCTRINE AND 2026 OFAC ENFORCEMENT

#### A. The Continuing Wrongs Principle Under ARSIWA Article 14

Under Article 14(2) of the Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts, a wrongful act of a continuing character extends over the entire period during which the act continues. In United States v. Dickinson, 331 U.S. 745 (1947) and Boling v. United States, 220 F.3d 1365 (Fed. Cir. 2000), the courts held that the statute of limitations does not begin to run until the continuous governmental taking has stabilized.

#### B. Active 2026 Sanctions Administration Prevents Accrual Stabilization

Because Defendants actively enforce 31 C.F.R. Part 515 every day in 2026—blocking transactions, freezing accounts, and prohibiting beneficial access—the taking and fiduciary breach continue in real time. The 28 U.S.C. § 2501 six-year limitations clock resets every 24 hours, completely defeating Defendants' time-bar defense.

### IV. SUN TZU ENTRAPMENT & JUDICIAL ESTOPPEL CHECKMATE

### V. RED TEAM REBUTTAL: PREEMPTION OF POLITICAL QUESTION & ACT OF STATE

#### A. Political Question Doctrine Inapplicable Under Baker and Zivotofsky

Under Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962) and Zivotofsky v. Clinton, 566 U.S. 189 (2012), claims seeking monetary compensation for constitutional takings are strictly justiciable.

#### B. Act of State Doctrine Precluded by Hickenlooper and Dunhill

Under 22 U.S.C. § 2370(e)(2) (Second Hickenlooper Amendment) and Alfred Dunhill of London, Inc. v. Republic of Cuba, 425 U.S. 682 (1976), the Act of State doctrine does not bar adjudication of confiscations violating international law or commercial acts of state.
