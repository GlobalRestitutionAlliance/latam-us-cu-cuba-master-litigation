### I. THE SUPREME COURT'S RESTRICTIVE MODERN DOCTRINE

#### A. The Baker v. Carr Six-Factor Framework

In Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186, 217 (1962), the Supreme Court established six narrow formulations of non-justiciable political questions. The Court cautioned that 'it is error to suppose that every case or controversy which touches foreign relations lies beyond judicial cognizance.'

#### B. Zivotofsky v. Clinton Restricts Foreign Policy Preemption

In Zivotofsky v. Clinton, 566 U.S. 189, 196 (2012), Chief Justice Roberts affirmed that federal courts cannot decline to decide a statutory or constitutional claim simply because it implicates foreign policy. The Judiciary's role is to interpret the law and determine property rights, even when the executive acts internationally.

### II. CONSTITUTIONAL TAKINGS AND FIDUCIARY TRUSTS ARE EXCLUSIVELY JUDICIAL MATTERS

#### A. Property Rights Are Anchored in Judicially Manageable Standards

Determining whether a taking occurred under the Fifth Amendment and calculating just compensation are traditional common-law functions governed by established standards (Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978)). No policy determination reserved to political branches is required.

#### B. Fiduciary Accounting Is Within Core Equity Jurisdiction

Enforcing trust obligations under United States v. Mitchell is a core judicial responsibility. The Court is not asked to sign a treaty or recognize a foreign government, but to determine whether the sovereign breached its trust while occupying Cuba.

#### Entrapment Mechanism:
