### I. INAPPLICABILITY OF ACT OF STATE TO UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT ACTS

#### A. The Scope of Banco Nacional de Cuba v. Sabbatino

The Act of State Doctrine (Banco Nacional de Cuba v. Sabbatino, 376 U.S. 398 (1964)) precludes courts from inquiring into the validity of the public acts of a recognized foreign sovereign committed within its own territory. It has zero application to the acts, orders, and regulations of the United States Government, its military governors, or OFAC.

#### B. Challenging U.S. Actions, Not Foreign Decrees

Plaintiffs' claims target the U.S. Military Government's decrees (1898–1902), the Platt Amendment, and active 2026 OFAC asset freezes. These are domestic acts of the defendant sovereign, fully subject to constitutional review under the Tucker Act.

### II. STATUTORY AND COMMON LAW EXCEPTIONS NEUTRALIZE SABBATINO

#### A. The Second Hickenlooper Amendment (22 U.S.C. § 2370(e)(2))

Congress expressly overruled the broader implications of Sabbatino by enacting 22 U.S.C. § 2370(e)(2), which commands that no court shall decline on the ground of the Act of State doctrine to make a determination on the merits giving effect to the principles of international law in a case involving an uncompensated confiscation.

#### B. The Commercial Activity Exception (Alfred Dunhill)

Under Alfred Dunhill of London, Inc. v. Republic of Cuba, 425 U.S. 682 (1976), purely commercial operations and sovereign trustee management of commercial assets are exempt from the doctrine.

#### C. The Bernstein Exception

Where the Executive branch explicitly or implicitly indicates that the doctrine need not apply to prevent injustice, courts adjudicate claims on the merits.
