### PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

Defendants will inevitably assert that the 221-year continuum (1805–2026) touches upon foreign policy, diplomatic recognition, and national security embargoes—matters they claim are strictly non-justiciable under the political question doctrine. This argument fundamentally confuses the *context* in which injuries arose with the *remedy* sought. 

Plaintiffs do not request this Court to sign a treaty, establish diplomatic recognition, lift an embargo, or dictate foreign policy to the Executive. Plaintiffs demand an adjudication of **discrete property rights**, the assessment of **uncompensated regulatory takings under the Fifth Amendment**, and compensation for the **breach of fiduciary duties** assumed during formal military and protectorate administrations. Under unbroken Supreme Court authority from *Baker v. Carr* to *Zivotofsky ex rel. Zivotofsky v. Clinton*, the judicial enforcement of private constitutional rights and trust duties remains the exclusive province of Article III courts, even when those rights intersect with international affairs.

### CONTROLLING LEGAL STANDARD: *BAKER v. CARR* AND ITS PROGENY

In *Baker v. Carr*, 369 U.S. 186, 217 (1962), the Supreme Court established the six definitive criteria determining the presence of a non-justiciable political question:
1. A textually demonstrable constitutional commitment of the issue to a coordinate political department;
2. A lack of judicially discoverable and manageable standards for resolving it;
3. The impossibility of deciding without an initial policy determination of a kind clearly for nonjudicial discretion;
4. The impossibility of a court’s undertaking independent resolution without expressing lack of the respect due coordinate branches of government;
5. An unusual need for unquestioning adherence to a political decision already made; or
6. The potentiality of embarrassment from multifarious pronouncements by various departments on one question.

Crucially, the Court explicitly cautioned that *"it is error to suppose that every case or controversy which touches foreign relations lies beyond judicial cognizance."* Id. at 211. In *Zivotofsky v. Clinton*, 566 U.S. 189, 196 (2012), the Supreme Court reaffirmed that when an individual claims a statutory or constitutional right, courts must decide the case: *"The federal courts are not being asked to supplant a foreign policy decision of the political branches with the courts’ own unmoored determination... The courts are called upon to perform a familiar judicial function: to determine whether [the action] violates a constitutional protection."*

### ARGUMENT

#### I. THE ADJUDICATION OF PROPERTY RIGHTS, CHAINS OF TITLE, AND FIFTH AMENDMENT JUST COMPENSATION IS TEXTUALLY COMMITTED TO THE JUDICIARY, NOT THE POLITICAL BRANCHES

The Fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution commands: *"[N]or shall private property be taken for public use, without just compensation."* The evaluation of whether a governmental act constitutes a taking, inverse condemnation, or regulatory destruction of private title is an Article III judicial mandate, textually committed to the courts.

1. **Constitutional Enforcement:** The Constitution does not commit the determination of Fifth Amendment takings to the President or to Congress; it commits it to the judiciary. See *United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256 (1946); *Armstrong v. United States*, 364 U.S. 40 (1960).
2. **Extraterritorial and Historic Property Claims:** The Court of Federal Claims and federal district courts have routinely adjudicated inverse condemnation claims arising in overseas territories, zones of occupation, and areas under U.S. military administration. See *Turney v. United States*, 115 F. Supp. 457 (Ct. Cl. 1953) (adjudicating taking claims involving property located in the Philippines); *Seery v. United States*, 127 F. Supp. 401 (Ct. Cl. 1955) (holding that the Fifth Amendment covers takings of alien-owned property abroad during military occupations).
3. **No Textual Immunity:** Nothing in Article II grants the Executive branch unreviewable power to extinguish private property rights or shield itself from just compensation under the banner of foreign policy.

#### II. PLAINTIFFS CHALLENGE DISCRETE EXECUTIVE AND ADMINISTRATIVE ACTIONS, NOT THE BROAD CONDUCT OF FOREIGN POLICY

Plaintiffs’ Master Complaint does not seek an advisory opinion on whether the embargo is prudent foreign policy, nor does it challenge the diplomatic wisdom of the Monroe Doctrine or the Platt Amendment. Rather, the Complaint challenges **concrete, operational regulatory intrusions** documented across Vectors 1 through 1805:
* The affirmative diversion and hypothecation of custom house receipts under early 20th-century receiverships (Vectors 251–500, 1451–1500);
* The direct issuance of zero-quota proclamations (Proclamation 3355) targeted at wiping out specific commercial agricultural property (Vectors 1551–1575);
* The continuous regulatory freeze of specific bank accounts and title registries under Executive Order 8389 and OFAC regulations that remain active in 2026 (Vectors 1601–1650).

As the Supreme Court held in *Chiles v. United States*, 69 F.3d 1094, 1096 (11th Cir. 1995), while the foreign policy apparatus is shielded from programmatic judicial review, specific, localized executive intrusions that inflict measurable economic injury upon private parties remain fully reviewable under standard administrative and constitutional frameworks.

#### III. JUDICIAL STANDARDS EXIST UNDER FEDERAL COMMON LAW, TRUST JURISPRUDENCE, AND INTERNATIONAL TAKINGS LAW TO ADJUDICATE FIDUCIARY BREACH AND CONTINUOUS WRONGS

Under *Baker*’s second prong, dismissal is appropriate only where there is a "lack of judicially discoverable and manageable standards." Here, the applicable standards are well-settled:

1. **Fiduciary Trust Standards:** Federal courts have centuries of established equity jurisprudence governing fiduciary duties. Under *United States v. Mitchell*, 463 U.S. 206 (1983), when the federal government exercises elaborate, direct control over land and assets belonging to a protected group or protectorate, a fiduciary duty is created as a matter of law. Evaluating whether U.S. military administrators prioritized Wall Street banking syndicates over Cuban property owners is a standard forensic accounting and fiduciary inquiry.
2. **Continuing Wrongs Doctrine:** The framework for determining whether continuous conduct tolls a statute of limitations is a familiar judicial tool. Under the International Law Commission’s Articles on State Responsibility (ARSIWA Article 14) and domestic analogues (*National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan*, 536 U.S. 101 (2002)), courts regularly distinguish between discrete acts and cumulative, continuous harms.
3. **Title and Valuation Standards:** The Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) and international arbitral tribunals (e.g., ICSID, PCA) have already developed precise, mathematical methodologies for valuing Cuban land, sugar centrals, and utility concessions (Vectors 751–800, 1651–1700). The Court need not invent standards; it need only apply those already codified in federal claims ledgers.

#### IV. THE POLITICAL QUESTION DOCTRINE CANNOT BE USED AS AN IMMUNIZING VEIL FOR CONTINUING CONSTITUTIONAL VIOLATIONS

Defendants’ anticipated invocation of the political question doctrine is an attempt to transform a doctrine of judicial restraint into an impenetrable shield of sovereign impunity. 

Where the Executive branch engages in an ongoing, multi-generational continuum of economic warfare that deliberately prevents the peaceful adjudication, sale, or enjoyment of real property, it cannot avoid Fifth Amendment accountability simply by whispering the words "foreign affairs." To permit dismissal under the political question doctrine would nullify the Constitution’s Takings Clause across any territory or asset class where the Executive has asserted geopolitical influence.
