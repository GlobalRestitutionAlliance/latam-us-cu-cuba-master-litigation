### I. MASTER PROPOSED FINDINGS OF FACT (1805–2026)

#### A. Findings 1–5: Antebellum Encroachment (1805–1860)

1. President Thomas Jefferson articulated designs for the acquisition of Cuba in 1805 and 1823.
2. In 1823, Secretary of State John Quincy Adams issued diplomatic instructions describing Cuba as a natural appendage of the U.S. (NARA RG 59).
3. In 1854, U.S. Ministers Buchanan, Soulé, and Mason drafted the Ostend Manifesto asserting the right to take Cuba by force (Vector 0001).

#### B. Findings 6–10: Military Occupation & Title Alterations (1898–1902)

4. On April 20, 1898, Congress passed the Teller Amendment (30 Stat. 738).
5. The Treaty of Paris (30 Stat. 1754, Art. I) placed Cuba under direct U.S. military governance.
6. Military Governor General Leonard Wood altered land registration laws via Civil Orders 62, 89, and 139 (NARA RG 140).
7. On October 29, 1901, General Wood admitted to President McKinley that 'There is, of course, no independence left in Cuba after the Platt Amendment. With the control which we have over its foreign relations and its financial affairs, and with the naval stations which we hold, the island is practically an American dependency, and we practically control the sugar trade of the world. (Major General Leonard Wood, Military Governor of Cuba, Letter to President William McKinley, Oct. 29, 1901, Leonard Wood Papers, Library of Congress Manuscript Division, Container 31).'.
8. Afro-Cuban troops under General Quintín Bandera were denied pensions and back pay by military decree (NARA RG 94).

#### C. Findings 11–15: Protectorate, FCSC & Modern OFAC Sanctions (1903–2026)

9. The Platt Amendment (31 Stat. 897) granted the U.S. supervisory dominion over Cuban treaties and treasury.
10. FCSC certified 5,911 claims totaling $1.8 billion under NARA RG 234.
11. In 2026, OFAC actively enforces 31 C.F.R. Part 515, continually blocking private assets.
