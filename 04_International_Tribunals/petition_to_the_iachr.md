### II. FACTUAL BACKGROUND: THE 221-YEAR CONTINUUM (VECTORS 1–1805)

#### A. Imperial Inception and Antebellum Latifundia Complicity (1805–1898)
5. From the earliest days of the American Republic, executive policy treated Cuba as a vital geopolitical and commercial prize. As documented in official state archives, President Thomas Jefferson declared in 1823 (*Library of Congress Jefferson Papers*) that Cuba was *"the most interesting addition which could ever be made to our system of states,"* while Secretary of State John Quincy Adams characterized the island as a *"natural appendage"* of North America (NARA Record Group 59).
6. Throughout the mid-19th century, North American capital, merchant factors, and financial syndicates industrialized Cuban sugar production, establishing vast monocultural latifundia, expanding the slave trade, and driving indigenous small farmers into intractable debt. Following the Ten Years' War (1868–1878) and the Treaty of Zanjón, U.S. capital absorbed distressed agrarian properties burdened by ruinous Spanish war taxes.
7. When the 1895 War of Independence erupted under José Martí, Máximo Gómez, and Antonio Maceo to establish an egalitarian republic ("Cuba Libre"), the Cleveland and McKinley administrations covertly supported Spanish sovereignty to protect U.S. investments. In June 1896, over one hundred planters petitioned President Cleveland for intervention because the independence movement threatened their property. On December 6, 1897, President McKinley urged in his First Annual Message that Spain be left free to conduct military operations while cutting off U.S. exile assistance to the insurgents. That same month, Undersecretary of War J.C. Breckinridge authored the December 1897 Breckinridge Memorandum declaring that the U.S. must *"clean up this country, even if this means using the methods Divine Providence used on the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah."*

#### B. Military Occupation, Fiduciary Breach, and Land Dispossession (1898–1902)
8. Following the April 20, 1898 Joint Resolution and the Teller Amendment (30 Stat. 738) disclaiming any intent to exercise permanent sovereignty over Cuba, United States military forces invaded the island. U.S. commanders systematically excluded the victorious Cuban Liberation Army from the surrender of Santiago de Cuba and barred all Cuban representatives from the August 1898 Treaty of Paris. Furthermore, U.S. authorities expended $3 million to pay off and disband General Máximo Gómez’s starving troops, dissolving the Cuban Revolutionary Party (PRC) and the provisional National Assembly.
9. During the subsequent military occupation (1898–1902), the U.S. Military Government under Governors-General John R. Brooke and Leonard Wood unilaterally revised the Cuban land title registration system (NARA Record Group 140). Because thirty years of war and Spanish reconcentration had destroyed municipal registries, thousands of Cuban smallholder families could not satisfy strict new documentary proof requirements. Their ancestral lands were seized and auctioned at distressed prices to North American corporations and land speculators—a transfer of wealth celebrated by the *Louisiana Planter* in 1903: *"Little by little the whole island is passing into the hands of American citizens."*
10. Simultaneously, occupation authorities dismantled the interracial democracy of the Cuban Liberation Army, systematically denying military commissions and full service back pay to Afro-Cuban officers—including General Quintín Bandera, a veteran commander since 1850—while locking the rural populace into landless wage peonage.
11. When Cuban voters defeated every U.S.-backed conservative candidate in the 1900 municipal and constituent assembly elections despite Secretary of War Elihu Root's restricted suffrage rules, Military Governor Leonard Wood complained to Senator Orville H. Platt on December 6, 1900 (NARA RG 94) that U.S. allies had been sidelined by *"the absolutely irresponsible and unreliable element."* Congress responded by coercing the Cuban Constitutional Convention into adopting the Platt Amendment (March 1901) under threat of perpetual military occupation. On October 29, 1901, General Wood admitted in a confidential letter to President William McKinley (**Library of Congress Manuscript Division**) that this fiduciary subversion was executed to secure a global sugar cartel: *"There is, of course, no independence left in Cuba after the Platt Amendment. With the control we now have over Cuba, combined with the other sugar producing lands we now own, we shall soon practically control the sugar trade of the world."*

#### C. Protectorate Interventions, Economic Warfare, and Active 2026 Sanctions
12. The neo-colonial latifundia structure engineered between 1898 and 1902 was militarily and financially enforced through subsequent U.S. interventions (1906–1909, 1912, 1921, 1933) and Wall Street loan indentures, directly provoking the structural collapse and confiscations of 1959.
13. Following the 1959 nationalizations, the United States initiated an unbroken regime of economic interdiction—memorialized in the April 6, 1960 Lester Mallory Memorandum (*FRUS, 1958–1960*, Vol. VI, p. 885) advocating economic strangulation to bring about hunger and regime overthrow, followed by Presidential Proclamations 3355 and 3447, the certification of $1.8 billion in FCSC claims (NARA RG 234), and continuous Treasury Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) asset freezes (31 C.F.R. Part 515) and Title III Helms-Burton enforcement (22 U.S.C. § 6082) operating actively in 2026.

### III. VIOLATIONS OF THE AMERICAN DECLARATION OF THE RIGHTS AND DUTIES OF MAN

#### A. Violation of Article XXIII (Right to Property) and Article X (Inviolability of Private Property)
14. Article XXIII of the American Declaration guarantees that *"every person has a right to own such private property as meets the essential needs of decent living and helps to maintain the dignity of the individual and of the home."*
15. The State Respondent violated this norm by: (a) utilizing military occupation decrees (1898–1902) to manipulate land registration standards and dispossess native smallholders of ancestral estates in favor of U.S. corporate sugar trusts; (b) imposing the coerced Platt Amendment to subordinate Cuban property rights to foreign commercial monopolies; and (c) instituting a continuous 66-year economic blockade and asset-freezing regime that renders Petitioners' property perpetually unmarketable and uncompensated.

#### B. Violation of Article II (Right to Equality Before Law) and Racial Discrimination
16. Article II establishes that *"all persons are equal before the law and have the rights and duties established in this Declaration, without distinction as to race, sex, language, creed or any other factor."*
17. The State Respondent violated Article II by importing post-*Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896) racial subjugation policies into occupied Cuba, systematically dismantling the interracial democracy of the Liberation Army, denying military commissions and back pay to Afro-Cuban veterans such as General Quintín Bandera, and subjugating Afro-Cuban populations to landless labor peonage.

#### C. Violation of Article XVIII (Right to a Fair Trial and Judicial Protection)
18. Article XVIII guarantees every person recourse to the courts to ensure protection against acts of violation of fundamental rights. The United States has violated Article XVIII by erecting procedural bars—specifically sovereign immunity, the 6-year Tucker Act statute of limitations, and the political question doctrine—that categorically deny Petitioners access to an effective judicial remedy in domestic courts for century-old structural takings and fiduciary breaches.
