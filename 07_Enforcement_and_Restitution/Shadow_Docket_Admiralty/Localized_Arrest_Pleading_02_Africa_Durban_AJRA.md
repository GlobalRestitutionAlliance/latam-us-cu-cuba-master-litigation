## 1. THE APPLICANTS AND MARITIME CLAIM

1.1 The Applicants are the Beneficial Proprietors of Historic Cuban Land and Commodity Muniments, asserting maritime claims within the meaning of Section 1(1) of the Admiralty Jurisdiction Regulation Act 105 of 1983 ('the Act').

1.2 The claim arises out of the wrongful conversion, carriage, and maritime exploitation of agricultural and financial assets belonging to the Applicants by the corporate successors of Chase National Bank and United Fruit Company.

## 2. THE 'ASSOCIATED SHIP' JURISDICTION UNDER SECTION 3(6) & 3(7)

2.1 Section 3(6) of the Act permits an action in rem to be brought by the arrest of an 'associated ship' instead of the ship in respect of which the maritime claim arose.

2.2 Section 3(7)(a)(ii) provides that an associated ship includes a ship owned by a company in which the shares are controlled, directly or indirectly, by the same parent conglomerate or corporate holding syndicate that controls the target debtor company.

2.3 The vessel M/V MORGAN SEAWAYS is ultimately owned and chartered through corporate holding structures controlled by the JPMorgan financial group, establishing perfect statutory association under South African admiralty law (*The Berg*, 1984 (4) SA 647 (A); *The Heavy Metal*, 1999 (1) SA 705 (SCA)).

## 3. URGENCY AND THE JURISDICTIONAL TRIGGER

3.1 The target vessel is presently within or approaching the territorial waters and port limits of the Port of Durban, KwaZulu-Natal.

3.2 If the vessel enters port and bunkers without being arrested, she will depart beyond the jurisdiction of this Court within twenty-four (24) hours, causing irreparable harm to the Applicants.

## 4. PRAYERS AND RELIEF SOUGHT

**4.1 That a Warrant for the Arrest in Rem of the M/V MORGAN SEAWAYS (IMO 9687441), her equipment, tackle, apparel, and freights, do issue forthwith.**

**4.2 That the Sheriff of this Court be directed to execute the warrant by boarding the vessel and affixing the warrant to the mast.**

**4.3 That the vessel be detained by the Transnet National Ports Authority (TNPA) and the Harbor Master until security in the sum of US$ 180,000,000.00 is lodged with the Registrar by way of cash or a first-class P&I Club Letter of Undertaking.**
