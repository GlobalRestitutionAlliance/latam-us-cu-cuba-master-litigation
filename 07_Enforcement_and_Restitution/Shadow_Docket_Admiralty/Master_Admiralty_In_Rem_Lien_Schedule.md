## I. ADMIRALTY JURISDICTION & THE IN REM 'PERSONIFICATION OF THE RES'

**A. JURISDICTION OVER THE MARITIME RES PURSUANT TO 28 U.S.C. § 1333 & SUPPLEMENTAL RULE C:**

This Court, sitting in Admiralty pursuant to Article III, Section 2 of the United States Constitution, 28 U.S.C. § 1333, and Rule C of the Supplemental Rules for Admiralty or Maritime Claims and Asset Forfeiture Actions, possesses plenary subject matter and in rem jurisdiction over the physical ocean-going vessels, cargo, freights, and equipment identified herein.

**B. THE TIMELESS DOCTRINE OF VESSEL PERSONIFICATION:**

Under ancient and immutable principles of maritime jurisprudence affirmed by the Supreme Court of the United States, an ocean-going vessel is treated as a distinct legal person capable of committing torts, incurring debts, and being arrested in rem (*The Brig Ann*, 13 U.S. (9 Cranch) 289 (1815); *The Palmyra*, 25 U.S. (12 Wheat.) 1 (1827); *The China*, 74 U.S. (7 Wall.) 53 (1868)). The maritime lien attaches directly to the hull, machinery, tackle, apparel, and cargo of the vessel the moment the underlying tortious conversion or breach of constructive trust occurs.

**C. THE COMMERCIAL INSTRUMENTS AND MARITIME LIENS ACT (CIMLA):**

Pursuant to 46 U.S.C. § 31342, any person providing necessaries or sustaining a maritime tort creates a maritime lien upon the vessel which may be enforced by an action in rem. Under 46 U.S.C. § 31326(b)(1), maritime tort liens for intentional conversion and fraudulent conveyance of beneficial property rank as 'preferred maritime liens', outranking even preferred ship mortgages held by global banking syndicates.

**COUNT II: CONSTRUCTIVE TRUST LIEN ATTACHING TO VESSEL HULL & CARGO PROCEEDS**

Under equitable principles of admiralty law and the law of constructive trusts (*In re Hallett's Estate* (1880) 13 Ch.D. 696; Restatement (Third) of Restitution § 55), property acquired with the proceeds of uncompensated expropriation remains impressed with an active trust. The modern vessels constructed, purchased, or chartered using historical surplus capital derived from Cuban latifundia are the physical embodiments of that trust, entitling Plaintiffs to arrest the res in rem.

**COUNT III: UNJUST ENRICHMENT & QUANTUM MERUIT UNDER GENERAL MARITIME LAW**

Defendants have extracted billions of dollars in freight revenues, charter hires, and commodity profits by trafficking in Plaintiffs' ancestral lands without paying a single dollar in restitution. General maritime law provides a direct in rem remedy against the vessels and cargo to disgorge these ill-gotten gains.

**COUNT IV: VIOLATION OF THE LAW OF NATIONS (PIRACY & UNCOMPENSATED HIGH-SEAS CONVERSION)**

Pursuant to 28 U.S.C. § 1350 and customary international maritime law, trafficking in confiscated goods upon international sea lanes violates the law of nations, subjecting the offending merchant vessels to universal maritime arrest in any port of call.
