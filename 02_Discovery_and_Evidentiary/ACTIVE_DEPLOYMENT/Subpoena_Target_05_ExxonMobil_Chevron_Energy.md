## II. HISTORICAL CORPORATE SCOPE & VECTOR MAPPING

Standard Oil Company of New Jersey (Esso / ExxonMobil), Texaco Inc. (Chevron), and Freeport Sulphur Co. (Freeport-McMoRan / Moa Bay Mining Co.) monopolized Cuba's petroleum refining and strategic mineral extraction sectors. In June–July 1960, under direct executive instructions from the U.S. State Department and CIA (the 'Mallory Doctrine'), Esso and Texaco simultaneously refused to refine crude oil imported by the Cuban government, deliberately precipitating the nationalization of the Havana Belot Refinery (FCSC CU-0064, $71.6M) and the Santiago de Cuba Refinery (FCSC CU-0077). Concurrently, Freeport Sulphur operated the Moa Bay nickel processing complex (FCSC CU-0027, $88.3M). All targets claimed 100% tax deductions from the U.S. Treasury under 26 U.S.C. § 165.
