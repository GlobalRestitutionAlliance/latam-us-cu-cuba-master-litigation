## I. STATUTORY PREDICATE & EXPANSIVE DEFINITION OF 'TRAFFICKING'
- **A. 22 U.S.C. § 6082(a)(1)(A) Civil Liability: ** The LIBERTAD Act provides that 'any person that... traffics in property which was confiscated by the Cuban Government on or after January 1, 1959, shall be liable to any United States national who owns the claim to such property for money damages.' Title III establishes private civil causes of action in federal district court with nationwide personal jurisdiction and worldwide enforcement reach.
- **B. 22 U.S.C. § 6023(13) Statutory Definition of Trafficking: ** Under federal law, a person 'traffics' in confiscated property if that person knowingly and intentionally: (i) sells, purchases, leases, receives, possesses, obtains control of, manages, uses, or holds an interest in confiscated property; (ii) engages in a commercial activity using or otherwise benefiting from confiscated property; or (iii) causes, directs, participates in, or profits from, trafficking by another person, or enters into a commercial arrangement to do so. This expansive definition captures not only physical operators on the ground in Cuba, but all international corporate parents, financial intermediaries, commodity brokers, and offshore holding companies that derive commercial benefit or financial returns from confiscated assets.
